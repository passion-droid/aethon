# Logo / monogram / branded entrance mats — manufacturers' guidance, print limits, and discreet-luxury practice

Research date: 2026-11-13. Scope: (a) logo-mat makers' technical and design rules, (b) legibility/print limits by technology, (c) how discreet luxury handles branding at thresholds — ending with applicability to the Paphos house (eight planned mats, stone-tone base, dark-green "AETHON" wordmark in a light sans with thin strokes and a dot above the O, logo ≈ 30 % of mat width, centred).

A note on source quality: almost everything written about logo mats is written by people who sell them. The only non-vendor voices found are one trade-press article (CleanLink, quoting a Crown Matting manager), one Forbo technical sheet (test data to ISO standards), and a 99% Invisible piece on ancient thresholds. Hospitality design press (Hospitality Design, Sleeper, Dezeen) does not cover doormats at all — see the Gaps under Key Question 4. Several EU manufacturer pages (Notrax/Justrite EU, Make An Entrance, The Mat Group's hospitality article) block automated fetches (HTTP 403); where I rely on search-engine snippets of those pages I say so.

## Key Question 1 — Logo size and placement rules from logo-mat makers

### Takeaway
No manufacturer publishes a "logo = X % of mat width" rule or a "keep within the central 60–70 %" rule; what they publish instead is a **maximum imprint area per mat size** (ranging from "the whole mat" to roughly half the width), a **minimum bleed of ~1 cm**, advice **against designing a border** (take a bigger mat instead), and an **orientation rule: the wordmark reads upright to the person arriving** (landscape across a doorway, portrait when the mat runs inward from the door).

### Cited Findings
- **Maximum imprint area (The Mat Group, Australia — Hog Heaven Impressions printed nylon):** 600 × 900 mm mat → max imprint 304 × 558 mm; 900 × 1500 → 609 × 1117; 1150 × 1800 → 914 × 1270 (i.e. the printable field is ≈ 51 % of the width and 62 % of the length on the smallest mat, ≈ 80 % × 70 % on the largest). Preferred artwork: Adobe Illustrator vector (.ai/.eps). "Light background colors should be avoided due to their tendency to show dirt on the mat more than a darker or neutral tone." — [TMG Design Guidelines, Hog Heaven Impressions Logo Mat (PDF)](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0565/5216/1447/files/TMG_Design_Guidelines_HogHeavenImpressionsLogoMat_AUS_email.pdf)
- **Maximum imprint area (Mountville / Logo Mats LLC — Grand Impressions HD):** imprint may cover the entire finished mat (e.g. 3' × 5' mat, finished 35" × 59", max imprint 35" × 59"); same light-background warning; "We also advise against the use of screens, tints and transparencies in the design because they are not easily converted to our color pallet." — [Grand Impressions HD Design Specifications (PDF)](https://s3.amazonaws.com/aws-website-mountvillecontent-zpj0f/Website-LMI/Product+Design+Specifications/Grand+Impressions+HD.pdf)
- **Logo may not touch the edge (SmartSign, Hog Heaven Xpressions):** "your logo or design can not extend to the edge of the mat"; a 3' × 5' mat has a maximum logo design of 24" × 44" (≈ 67 % × 73 % of the mat). — [SmartSign Hog Heaven custom logo mats](https://www.smartsign.com/hog-heaven-custom-logo-mats)
- **Bleed and borders (Notrax / BrilliantStep EU artwork specs — page returns 403 to direct fetch; figures from search snippets):** minimum space around the design (bleed) at least 1 cm; a designed border is not recommended — "if you want one, take a larger size mat"; avoid "many small shapes and fine lines" as they "could disappear or print very broken and rough on smaller mat sizes"; lighter areas "appear dirty faster". — [buyjustrite.eu logo-mat artwork specifications](https://www.buyjustrite.eu/en/logo-mat-artwork-specifications/)
- **Edge-to-edge is possible on printed vinyl mesh** (American Floor Mats, "DirectPrint" vinyl-loop): "the logo can extend edge-to-edge"; no margins or max-logo sizes are given. — [American Floor Mats, Vinyl Mesh Printed Logo Mats](http://www.americanfloormats.com/vinyl-mesh-printed-logo-mats/)
- **Orientation — the only placement rule that recurs across makers:** "Choose the landscape orientation if the mat will be placed across an entryway in a horizontal fashion, as this will display the logo and slogan in an upright and coherent manner. On the other hand, choose a portrait orientation if the mat is to extend inward from an entryway in a vertical fashion." Also: guests "will have only a few seconds to take notice of the design, slogan, or logo." — [Eagle Mat blog, logo-mat design tips](https://www.eaglemat.com/blog/?p=373)
- **Trade press (CleanLink, quoting Adam Strizzi, marketing manager, Crown Matting Technologies):** keep the design simple because "people are going to be walking over the mat and you only have a few seconds to catch their attention"; choose horizontal vs vertical by where the mat goes; a long landscape design "works best in the middle of a large entryway"; background should complement the logo, dark background for a light logo. — [CleanLink, What To Consider When Creating Logo Mats](https://www.cleanlink.com/news/article/What-To-Consider-When-Creating-Logo-Mats--19367)
- **Facing the arriving person:** a vendor design guide surfaced in search advises that "in a narrow hallway, a portrait orientation with the logo facing the approaching guest is best" and that the logo must "be seen in the correct angle when customers enter the establishment" (search snippets; exact page not verified). — [Personalized Doormats, tips & tricks for designing custom logo mats](https://personalizeddoormats.com/blogs/news/tips-and-tricks-for-designing-your-custom-logo-mats)
- **Sizing the mat to the opening:** "A mat that is too small may fail to capture dirt and moisture effectively," an oversized mat "can create layout issues"; measure the entrance. — [Personalized Doormats, common mistakes when choosing logo floor mats](https://personalizeddoormats.com/blogs/news/common-mistakes-when-choosing-logo-floor-mats)
- **Hotel logo-mat sizes offered (UK):** standard 60 × 40 cm up to 200 × 200 cm; custom up to 200 cm wide × 650 cm long. — [First Mats, hotel lobby logo mats](https://www.firstmats.co.uk/products/hotel-lobby-logo-mats)

### Inferences
- The planned 30 %-of-width wordmark sits comfortably inside every published imprint limit (the tightest found is ≈ 51 % of width on a 600 × 900 printed mat); the limits bite only if the logo were to approach the edge. "One logo per mat, centred" is not written down anywhere, but every maker's proofing workflow and every max-imprint table assumes a single centred artwork field.
- Reading direction: makers' rule is "upright to the person approaching". Applied to a door seen from both sides, that means: street/kitchen door mats read upright to someone arriving from outside; the indoor mats inside the front door read upright to someone who has just stepped in (portrait "extending inward"); on balconies with no external access the "arriving" person is the one stepping out of the room, so the wordmark reads upright from inside the room. No maker addresses the from-inside case explicitly — this is my application of their rule.

### Gaps
- No manufacturer rule expressing logo size as a percentage of mat width, nor a "central 60–70 %" safe zone, was found in any artwork guide (Mountville, TMG, Notrax, Uline, Forbo, COBA, Kleen-Tex, First Mats). The de-facto bounds are max-imprint tables and the ≈ 1 cm bleed.
- Kleen-Tex (Jet-Print / Logomat) publishes no artwork-size rules on its product page (see KQ2); Milliken's logo-mat artwork spec was not found (the "Classic Impressions" line is now credited to M+A Matting).

## Key Question 2 — Print-technology limits: minimum letter height, stroke width, colour tolerance, contrast, colour count, thin wordmarks

### Takeaway
Minimum stroke widths span **two orders of magnitude by technology**: ≈ 1.5–3 mm on HD dye-injected/jet-printed cut-pile nylon, ≈ 7 mm on printed Berber loop nylon, ≈ 10 mm on inlaid vinyl-coil ("spaghetti") mats; minimum text heights run 6–13 mm (HD nylon) → 40–50 mm (Berber / coil inlay) → 76 mm (US coil inlay). Every spec sheet that gives numbers adds the same sentence: **"special fonts and very thin letter forms may need to be made larger or thicker"**. Pantone matching is offered everywhere and guaranteed nowhere ("a few shades off" is the honest wording); no maker publishes a ΔE tolerance. Light backgrounds are discouraged by every maker on dirt grounds, not legibility grounds.

### Cited Findings

**Printed / dye-injected cut-pile nylon (the "HD" class — Chromojet / jet-print / layered colour):**
- Mountville Grand Impressions HD (indoor, medium traffic): **minimum text height 1" (25 mm) for serif fonts and ½" (12.7 mm) for sans serif; minimum line thickness ⅛" (3.2 mm); white and light colours at least ¼" (6.35 mm)**; "produced using a layering process of color … matched to our standard color palette of over 150 colors unless a specific coated PMS color is requested"; "The text requirements are for general use; however, special fonts and very thin letter forms may need to be made larger or thicker and will be approached on a case-by-case basis." — [Grand Impressions HD spec (PDF)](https://s3.amazonaws.com/aws-website-mountvillecontent-zpj0f/Website-LMI/Product+Design+Specifications/Grand+Impressions+HD.pdf)
- The Mat Group, Hog Heaven Impressions (AU): **minimum text height 6 mm; minimum line thickness 1.5 mm**; colours matched to the maker's own range; "Tints, screens, and transparencies are not supported"; same thin-letterform caveat. — [TMG Hog Heaven Impressions guidelines (PDF)](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0565/5216/1447/files/TMG_Design_Guidelines_HogHeavenImpressionsLogoMat_AUS_email.pdf)
- Personalized Doormats "Floor Impressions" (printed): minimum text ¼" (6.35 mm) on the diamond surface, ⅛" (3.2 mm) on smooth; minimum line 1/16" (1.6 mm) (search snippet of the spec). — [Show Your Logo, Floor Impressions 3' × 5'](https://www.showyourlogo.com/product/floor-impressions-indoor-floor-mat-3-x-5/)
- M+A Matting ColorStar Impressions HD (printed PET, via distributor): minimum line ⅛" (3.2 mm), ¼" (6.35 mm) for white/light colours (search snippet). — [Gotopac, ColorStar Impressions HD](https://www.gotopac.com/m-a-matting-60.html)
- Kleen-Tex Jet-Print Vision: "high-definition dye-injection printing" (technical section: "Chromojet printing"); standard palette plus **RGB colour matching and Pantone matching available**; claims "fine detail, photographic colour precision, and sharp reproduction of gradients, fine lines"; solution-dyed straight-set nylon on nitrile rubber; washable 30–60 °C; claims "fade-resistant" with **no lightfastness rating given**; listed applications are Events / Point of Sale / Advertising — **no outdoor claim**; **no artwork minima published**. — [Kleen-Tex, Jet-Print Vision](https://kleen-tex.com/products/jet-print-vision)
- Forbo Coral Logo (printed version): "Hi-definition custom print" on 100 % polyamide tufted cut pile; "Choose from any of the colours shown plus **Pantone and RAL colour matching**"; the printed version is "Perfect for complicated designs featuring multiple colours", the cut-inset version is "Ideal for extreme traffic conditions"; "Ideal for inside entrance and circulation areas". **No minimum text/line figures** on the sheet. — [Forbo Coral Logo technical specs 2016 (PDF)](https://forbo.blob.core.windows.net/forbodocuments/1767703/Forbo_Coral_Logo_technical_specs_2016.pdf)
- COBA Logomat (UK, jet-printed nylon 6.6): palette "up to 66 colours" (the downloads section says 40), **maximum 6 colours per mat**; Pantone matching not mentioned on the page; no minimum text/line stated. — [COBA Logomat](https://www.coba.com/product/logomat); COBA's buying guide distinguishes the jet-printed Logomat from the robot-precision-cut inlaid Precision Loop, and says its design team "will finalise and refine your design" before you approve a proof. — [COBA, How to design a logo mat](https://www.coba.com/buying-guides/how-to-design-a-logo-mat)
- First Mats (UK, HD-printed polyamide): "True Pantone and HEX colour matching is included free as standard, with unlimited colour options"; darker backgrounds "best for hiding dirt"; 5-year guarantee; wash ≤ 30 °C delicate, "dry flat, out of direct sunlight"; **no resolution or minimum-size figures**. — [First Mats, logo mats](https://www.firstmats.co.uk/pages/logo-mats)
- Lower-resolution printed carpet (Uline, twisted polyester): "digitally printed … at a crisp **76 DPI**"; "Can print text as small as **2" tall**" (51 mm); artwork rule: avoid "Fine lines, CMYK colors or photographs"; submit ≥ 300 dpi (600 preferred), outline all fonts. Also: "**Some brands do not allow their logo to be printed on a floor mat for any reason.**" — [Uline Custom Printed Carpet Mat Guide (PDF)](https://img.uline.com/is/content/uline/Custom_Printed_Carpet_Mat_Guide_PDF_ENUS)
- Notrax/BrilliantStep EU (search snippets; page 403s): minimum pixel width 10 for text and fine lines (1 px suffices for black); "the closest Pantone match for a mat's surface is the **Uncoated (U)** colour line"; Notrax's catalogue states that "each colour prints differently on each mat because of differences in texture and surface quality". — [buyjustrite.eu artwork specs](https://www.buyjustrite.eu/en/logo-mat-artwork-specifications/); [Notrax custom printed logo mats catalogue (PDF, 403 to fetch)](https://notrax.justrite.com/media/mageplaza/product_attachments/attachment_file/n/o/notrax-custom-printed-logo-mats_nt346_web.pdf)

**Printed Berber / loop-pile nylon:**
- The Mat Group, Printed Berber Logo Mat: "**All text height must be a minimum of 40 mm. Line thickness must be a minimum of 7 mm.**" Same thin-letterform and light-background caveats; tints/screens/transparencies not supported. — [TMG Printed Berber guidelines (PDF)](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0565/5216/1447/files/TMG_Design_Guidelines_PrintedBerberLogoMat_Jan24_email.pdf)

**Printed rubber / scraper:**
- Canada Mats Superscrape Impressions (printed rubber): minimum text ½" (12.7 mm) incl. lowercase; minimum line 1/16" (1.6 mm); ≥ 200 dpi (search snippet). — [Canada Mats, Superscrape Impressions](https://canadamats.ca/superscrape-impressions.html)
- Personalized Doormats WaterHog Impressions HD (printed rubber-backed polypropylene, indoor/outdoor): minimum text 1.5" (38 mm), minimum line ¼" (6.35 mm) (search snippet). — [SPEC_WIM (PDF)](https://personalizeddoormats.com/cdn/shop/files/SPEC_WIM.pdf)
- TMG PrintScraper (printed nylon scraper, outside or inside): "High-quality dyes offer brilliant colour fastness and image durability"; no rating, no artwork minima. — [TMG PrintScraper spec (PDF)](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0565/5216/1447/files/Printscraper_logo_Spec_Sheet_lowres.pdf)

**Vinyl-loop / coil ("spaghetti", "mesh") mats — the technology planned for the two street-side doors:**
- Personalized Doormats StrandShield (vinyl loop, inlaid): "there are **very strict design requirements** for this product … **Minimum Text Height: 3" (76 mm) (includes lower case text). Minimum Line Thickness: 3/8" (9.5 mm). Any space between letters or design elements must be at minimum 3/8"**"; "Gradients, tints, screens and transparencies are not supported"; "White material should not be used as the background color or for large sections of the mat as it is difficult to keep the color clean"; UV & water resistant; 7 standard colours; fully drainable, no backing. — [StrandShield spec (PDF)](https://personalizeddoormats.com/cdn/shop/files/SPEC_SSVL.pdf)
- The Mat Group SpiralLoop Inlay (NZ): text and symbols **at least 50 mm high and 10 mm thick; outlines ≥ 10 mm; spacing ≥ 10 mm** (search summary). — [TMG SpiralLoop Inlay guidelines (PDF)](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0565/5216/1447/files/TMG_Design_Guidelines_SpiralLoopInlayMat_EMAIL.pdf)
- Mats Inc. Spaghetti Logo Mats: logo "is inlaid into the coil instead of sitting on top as a print"; **19 standard colours** (incl. sand, beige, gray); indoor or outdoor; "resists mildew and fading", no rating; 1-year limited warranty; light-traffic scraper. — [Mats Inc., spaghetti logo mats](https://mats-inc.com/product/logo-mats/all-logo-mats/spaghetti-logo-mats.html)
- American Floor Mats Vinyl Mesh **Printed** Logo Mats (½" extruded vinyl loop, "DirectPrint"): "no limit" to colours, shading possible; 23-colour chart incl. Sandstone, Beige, Gunmetal; "UV stabilizers help them resist fading, including outdoors or in direct sunlight"; **no minimum text/line figures published** — the only printed-coil spec found gives none. — [American Floor Mats, vinyl mesh printed logo mats](http://www.americanfloormats.com/vinyl-mesh-printed-logo-mats/)

**Colour-matching tolerance (what makers actually promise):**
- Mountville: "We will do our very best to match a PMS color … however, color matching can be challenging. Although we can get very close, color is never an exact science and variations will occur … we are printing on white, nylon carpet … we use a hot press to combine the carpet and rubber backing. The heat from the press often causes the color to lighten. Typically, we can get close to most PMS colors, but **the final color may be a few shades off**." — [Grand Impressions HD spec (PDF)](https://s3.amazonaws.com/aws-website-mountvillecontent-zpj0f/Website-LMI/Product+Design+Specifications/Grand+Impressions+HD.pdf)
- Promotional-print houses use the same disclaimer language: cannot "warrant a Pantone match" across materials, will "come as close as possible". — [GoPromotional artwork tips](https://gopromotional.co.uk/printing-artwork-tips); [Allwag Pantone guide](https://www.allwag.co.uk/bespoke.aspx?besID=384)
- Counter-claim: First Mats advertises "true Pantone colour matching" (marketing copy, no tolerance stated). — [First Mats](https://www.firstmats.co.uk/pages/logo-mats)
- "Colors may also look different depending on the material and printing process used"; a "small typo, incorrect color shade, or stretched logo" is costly, so check the proof. — [Personalized Doormats, common mistakes](https://personalizeddoormats.com/blogs/news/common-mistakes-when-choosing-logo-floor-mats)

**Contrast and backgrounds:**
- "Use contrasting colors that will help your logo 'pop'"; dark logo on light background or light logo on dark. — [Eagle Mat](https://www.eaglemat.com/blog/?p=373)
- Every spec sheet above repeats "light background colors should be avoided" — the reason given is always **dirt visibility**, never legibility. — [Mountville](https://s3.amazonaws.com/aws-website-mountvillecontent-zpj0f/Website-LMI/Product+Design+Specifications/Grand+Impressions+HD.pdf); [TMG](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0565/5216/1447/files/TMG_Design_Guidelines_HogHeavenImpressionsLogoMat_AUS_email.pdf); [First Mats](https://www.firstmats.co.uk/pages/logo-mats)
- "Small text, thin lines, and overly detailed artwork can become difficult to read once printed"; "Complex logos with fine details may not translate well onto mat surfaces." — [Personalized Doormats, common mistakes](https://personalizeddoormats.com/blogs/news/common-mistakes-when-choosing-logo-floor-mats)

### Inferences
- Technology ladder for a thin light-sans wordmark (minimum stroke → minimum cap height): HD dye-injected nylon **1.5–3.2 mm → 6–13 mm**; printed Berber **7 mm → 40 mm**; printed rubber **1.6–6.35 mm → 13–38 mm**; coil/vinyl-loop inlay **9.5–10 mm → 50–76 mm**. Printed (not inlaid) vinyl-loop has **no published minima**; given the ½" open coil surface, assume the inlay numbers as the safe working bound until a maker proves otherwise with a strike-off.
- "Set bolder or larger": no maker says "use the bold weight" outright, but every one reserves the right to thicken or enlarge "very thin letter forms" case-by-case — i.e. the thin wordmark will be redrawn by the mat printer unless the owner pre-empts it with a heavier cut or a larger size.
- The 2-colour plan (base + one print colour) is well inside every colour budget (COBA caps at 6/mat; Mountville 150+ palette). Pantone: specify the **Uncoated (U)** reference and the nearest standard-palette chip as a fallback; expect the pressed, heat-bonded nylon to come out lighter than the chip; insist on a physical sample before the eight-piece run.

### Gaps
- No ΔE or numeric Pantone tolerance from any mat maker; "a few shades off" (Mountville) is the only quantified-in-words statement.
- No printed vinyl-loop (as opposed to inlaid) minimum text/stroke figure exists in public specs (American Floor Mats, Mats Inc. and Forbo all omit it).
- Kleen-Tex Jet-Print and emco: no public artwork minima found; Kleen-Tex offers Pantone/RGB matching but publishes no DPI or line-width rule. Milliken: no artwork spec found.

## Key Question 3 — Fading and wear of printed logos; replacement cycles in hospitality rental schemes

### Takeaway
Only one maker publishes a lightfastness test value for a printed logo mat: **Forbo Coral Logo (printed) rates 7 on the EN ISO 105-B02 blue-wool scale** (COBA Logomat: 5, with "UV resistance: No" and a 2-year fade warranty). US printed-carpet logo mats are explicitly "not intended for use in direct sunlight as fading will occur"; UV-stabilised vinyl-loop and solution-dyed fibre are the outdoor-rated constructions. Rental schemes exchange mats on a traffic-based cadence and replace them "when worn" — no fixed interval for logo mats is published by Cintas, Elis, Initial or CWS.

### Cited Findings
- **Forbo Coral Logo:** colour fastness to light EN ISO 105/B02 = **7** (printed) and **> 5** (cut-inset 43xx); rubbing EN ISO 105/X12 4–5; water EN ISO 105/E01 4–5; the printed version is described as "Long lasting and high resistance to sun light" and the product is positioned for "inside entrance and circulation areas". Marketing text on the same sheet: "How often have you seen logo mats that look dirty, faded, worn? That's what happens when a logo mat is treated as a bit of decoration rather than a serious element in interior design … A logo mat shouldn't be a gimmick." — [Forbo Coral Logo technical specs 2016 (PDF)](https://forbo.blob.core.windows.net/forbodocuments/1767703/Forbo_Coral_Logo_technical_specs_2016.pdf)
- **COBA Logomat:** colour fastness to light rated **5**; "UV resistance: No"; 2-year guarantee against manufacturing defects plus a separate **2-year warranty covering fading**; rubbing/water/soiling fastness 5. — [COBA Logomat](https://www.coba.com/product/logomat)
- **Printed Berber (US, Berber Impressions HD) — explicit sun warning:** "These mats are intended for indoor or outdoor covered use; **not intended for use in direct sunlight as fading will occur**" (search snippet). — [Global Industrial, Berber Impressions HD](https://www.globalindustrial.com/p/berber-impressions-hd-custom-logo-mat-2-x-3-68-mil-universal-ceated-sbr)
- **Vinyl-loop printed (outdoor):** "UV stabilizers help them resist fading, including outdoors or in direct sunlight" (vendor claim, no rating). — [American Floor Mats](http://www.americanfloormats.com/vinyl-mesh-printed-logo-mats/); Mats Inc.'s inlaid coil "resists mildew and fading", 1-year warranty. — [Mats Inc.](https://mats-inc.com/product/logo-mats/all-logo-mats/spaghetti-logo-mats.html)
- **Solution-dyed vs printed:** a UK outdoor logo mat is sold on "Solution Dyed fibers for 100% UV-resistance" (search snippet) — i.e. colour in the fibre rather than on it is the outdoor route. — [Mats4U outdoor heavy-duty logo mat](https://mats4u.co.uk/products/outdoor-heavy-duty-logo-mat)
- **First Mats care sheet:** machine wash ≤ 30 °C, "Dry flat, out of direct sunlight, and don't tumble dry"; 5-year guarantee; "designed to look as good on day 1,000 as they did on day one" (marketing). — [First Mats](https://www.firstmats.co.uk/pages/logo-mats)
- **Kleen-Tex Jet-Print:** "withstands repeated commercial laundering without fading" (claim; no rating), wash 30–60 °C, tumble dry medium. — [Kleen-Tex](https://kleen-tex.com/products/jet-print-vision)
- **A rival vendor's blunt lifespan view (Rugster, EU):** standard generic logo mats "last 6 to 12 months"; outdoors "The print fades, dirt darkens the fibres around the design, and within a year it looks worse than no mat at all"; an unprinted mat "stays visually clean longer than any logo mat under direct sun and rain"; "Save the logo mat for inside"; coir "disintegrates within 12 months under any real foot traffic"; PVC backing "cracks within a winter". (Vendor blog; figures uncorroborated.) — [Rugster, logo mat buying guide](https://www.rugster.eu/blogs/news/business-logo-mat-buying-guide)
- **Rental cadence:** Cintas "Deliver[s] fresh, clean mats" with service frequency "customized based on traffic levels"; the only fixed figure on its pages is restroom mats working "for up to 28 days" (not logo mats). — [Cintas commercial mat rental](https://www.cintas.com/local/commercial-mat-rental); Elis says its service "supports longevity of products", no timeline. — [Elis UK floor protection mats](https://uk.elis.com/en/services/floor-protection-mats); Cleantex (AU) delivers fresh mats "every week, every other week" and replaces mats when worn or damaged. — [SWS Cleantex floor mats](https://www.swsgroup.com.au/cleantex/floor-mats); Lindström: change mats more often in the wet season. — [Lindström mat service](https://lindstromgroup.com/renewal-site-template/?p=16722)
- **Unverified vendor comparison:** rental logo mats carry "3–6 months of warranty with limited or no performance guarantee", purchased mats 1–2 years. — [Ultimate Mats, rental vs purchase lifecycle analysis](https://ultimatemats.com/?p=53294)

### Inferences
- Blue-wool 7 (Forbo) is a strong indoor rating; blue-wool 5 (COBA) plus "UV: No" is the typical jet-print nylon reality — fine under cover, not for an uncovered Mediterranean balcony. Nobody tests a printed nylon logo mat for outdoor UV because nobody rates it for outdoors.
- For the Paphos climate the fade ranking is: inlaid or solution-dyed colour (vinyl-loop inlay, solution-dyed fibre) > UV-stabilised printed vinyl-loop > printed nylon under full cover > printed nylon in sun (expect visible loss within a season or two — the Rugster "within a year" figure is a vendor's number, but the Global Industrial "not for direct sunlight" warning is a maker's).
- Hospitality rental is a hygiene cadence (weekly–monthly swap) with replacement on wear; the visible logo is kept fresh by rotation, not by lightfast printing — a residence buying its mats outright has no such rotation, so lightfastness and the choice to brand outdoor mats matter more.

### Gaps
- No published fade-test data for Chromojet/Jet-Print (Kleen-Tex), TMG, Mountville or any printed vinyl-loop mat; only Forbo and COBA give a blue-wool number.
- No rental company states how long a logo mat stays in rotation before retirement; "3–6 months" is a competitor's claim.

## Key Question 4 — How discreet luxury handles branding at thresholds; the residential monogram tradition; "corporate" logo mats vs monograms

### Takeaway
Text sources document **no** entrance-mat practice for Aman, Six Senses, Four Seasons, Rosewood or Belmond, and the design press is silent on doormats; what can be sourced is (i) the written design principle that a clean interior takes a clean mat ("A clean lobby gets a clean mat. A branded interior gets a branded mat"), (ii) the fact that brand owners often forbid their logo underfoot at all, (iii) the heritage/estate tradition of a house name or crest on coir, with tonal techniques (heat-embossed, laser-burnt) available where restraint is wanted, and (iv) a 2,000-year lineage of inscribed thresholds (HAVE at the House of the Faun) that frames the threshold mark as a greeting set by the household, not an advertisement.

### Cited Findings
- **The only design-voice rule found:** "a clean solid-colour entrance mat may suit better than a branded one" for "heritage buildings, high-end interior design offices, minimalist retail"; "**A clean lobby gets a clean mat. A branded interior gets a branded mat.**" — [Rugster, logo mat buying guide](https://www.rugster.eu/blogs/news/business-logo-mat-buying-guide)
- **Brands that refuse to be walked on:** a mat printer's artwork rules note, "Some brands do not allow their logo to be printed on a floor mat for any reason." — [Uline Custom Printed Carpet Mat Guide (PDF)](https://img.uline.com/is/content/uline/Custom_Printed_Carpet_Mat_Guide_PDF_ENUS)
- **"Gimmick" warning from a premium manufacturer:** "A logo mat shouldn't be a gimmick – it should perform as well as it looks." — [Forbo Coral Logo specs (PDF)](https://forbo.blob.core.windows.net/forbodocuments/1767703/Forbo_Coral_Logo_technical_specs_2016.pdf)
- **Hotel practice that IS documented is budget/mid-market:** Travelodge replaced rental mats with a bespoke lobby logo mat "made to brand guidelines and Pantone references" (search snippet). — [First Mats, Travelodge case study](https://www.firstmats.co.uk/blogs/case-studies/travelodge-replaces-rental-mats-with-bespoke-branded-entrance-solution); hotel supply houses sell rubber/nylon mats "with your hotel name or logo imprinted". — [Western Hotel Supply, hotel entrance mats](https://westernhotelsupply.com/collections/hotel-entrance-mats); vendor guidance for hotels puts logo mats at "Entrance and Lobby Areas – first point of contact", often repeated at reception, with the name "readable from a distance" (vendor blog). — [Business Mat Branding, logo mats for hotels](https://businessmatbranding.blogspot.com/2025/02/logo-mats-for-hotels-how-to-choose.html)
- **Heritage / estate coir tradition (UK):** a maker of hand-stitched coir for period buildings, marketed under an English Heritage association, invites you to "Choose a mat with your house name, logo or crest" (search snippet; page 403s to fetch). — [Make An Entrance, English Heritage traditional coir matting](https://www.makeanentrance.com/englishheritage)
- **Estate monogram as a retail category (US):** "Estate Monogrammed Coco Door Mat". — [Frontgate](https://www.frontgate.com/estate-monogrammed-coco-door-mat/545266); "Personalize your estate doormat with a single-letter or three-letter monogram." — [Personalized Doormats, estate doormats](https://personalizeddoormats.com/collections/estate-doormats); "The Monogrammed Imperial Grade Coir Mat". — [Hammacher Schlemmer](https://www.hammacher.com/product/monogrammed-imperial-grade-coir-mat)
- **Tonal / no-pigment techniques exist:** an embossed coir mat whose word is "embossed using heat and pressure – without using any dyes or pigments". — [Lowe's, Style Selections embossed coir mat](https://www.lowes.com/pd/Style-Selections-18x30-Embossed-Home-Sustainable-Coir-Mat/5000178085); a South African maker burns the monogram into coir fibres with a laser so it "cannot be worn away by heavy foot traffic", while noting that coir's coarse fibre "limits fine detail, so intricate monograms may not reproduce well" (search snippets). — [Woven Bliss, entrance mats](https://www.wovenbliss.co.za/entrance-mats/); inlaid (cut-and-pieced) coir logos bonded to rubber are the premium coir method. — [Canada Mats, custom logo coir doormats](https://canadamats.ca/blog/custom-logo-coir-doormats-from-canada-mats/)
- **Inlaid is positioned above printed as the "premium" look** by several vendors (seller claims): Forbo's cut-inset Coral Logo for "extreme traffic"; Mats Inc.'s coil logo "inlaid … instead of sitting on top as a print"; COBA's robot-cut Precision Loop. — [Forbo](https://forbo.blob.core.windows.net/forbodocuments/1767703/Forbo_Coral_Logo_technical_specs_2016.pdf); [Mats Inc.](https://mats-inc.com/product/logo-mats/all-logo-mats/spaghetti-logo-mats.html); [COBA](https://www.coba.com/buying-guides/how-to-design-a-logo-mat)
- **The ancient lineage of the inscribed threshold:** the stoop of Pompeii's House of the Faun carries HAVE ("welcome") in coloured tesserae; classicist Sarah Bond: "doorways and thresholds were an important locus of power in Greco-Roman antiquity", and certain words "could serve to set the tone for guests entering a household"; modern doormats "still also serve that same ancient function, helping define the threshold space between public exteriors and private interiors." — [99% Invisible, Ancient Thresholds](https://99percentinvisible.org/article/ancient-thresholds-classical-welcome-mats-dazzling-medusa-mosaics/); the HAVE mosaic described. — [MIT, House of the Faun — Entrance](https://web.mit.edu/21h.405/www/HouseOfFaun/Entrance.html)
- **Fine detail on coir is a known limit** (coarse fibre), which is why the residential monogram tradition is a single bold letter or a short name in a heavy face, not a fine wordmark. — [Woven Bliss](https://www.wovenbliss.co.za/entrance-mats/) (snippet)

### Inferences
- The absence is itself the finding: luxury hotel entrances are photographed constantly, yet no text source describes an Aman/Four Seasons/Rosewood logo mat, while budget chains (Travelodge, Baymont) are documented logo-mat users. Combined with the Rugster principle and the "brands that forbid their logo on floors" note, the defensible reading is that discreet luxury either leaves the threshold unbranded (stone, recessed matwell, plain coir) or marks it tonally; a printed corporate-style logo mat is a mid-market signifier.
- The residential tradition (Roman HAVE → English estate coir with house name or crest) legitimises a **house name at the primary door** as a greeting from the household; it does not legitimise repeating it at every secondary opening.

### Gaps
- No documented entrance-mat practice for Aman, Six Senses, Four Seasons, Rosewood or Belmond (searched by brand + "doormat/entrance mat", and via hotel-standards and design-press queries); no brand-guideline PDF for physical floor applications was found for any luxury group. Hospitality Design / Sleeper / Dezeen returned nothing on mats.
- No published critique by a named interior designer of "corporate" logo mats; the Rugster line is a vendor's editorial.
- The Make An Entrance and Woven Bliss pages could not be fetched directly (403); their claims rest on search snippets.

## Key Question 5 — Is a logo on all eight mats excessive? Should only the primary threshold be branded?

### Takeaway
No source states a numeric limit, but the balance of guidance points one way: vendors (who sell mats) recommend branding main *and* secondary doors, while the only non-sales design principle found says a clean interior takes a clean mat, the only EU vendor to address outdoor logos says to keep them inside, and the hospitality rental model shows branded mats are kept presentable by frequent rotation that a residence will not have.

### Cited Findings
- **Vendor position (more branding):** "Use commercial entry mats at main doors, personalized business door mats at secondary entries, and branded mats in elevator lobbies and corridors." — [Personalized Doormats, why every business needs a branded doormat](https://personalizeddoormats.com/blogs/news/why-every-business-needs-a-branded-doormat-at-the-entrance-and-inside); another supplier: "position outside main and secondary doors … pair with interior custom floor mats with logo for a two-step system" (search snippet). — [Mats Inc., logo mats](https://mats-inc.com/product/logo-mats.html)
- **Restraint position:** "A clean lobby gets a clean mat. A branded interior gets a branded mat." and "Save the logo mat for inside." — [Rugster](https://www.rugster.eu/blogs/news/business-logo-mat-buying-guide)
- **Attention budget:** visitors "generally will have only a few seconds to take notice of the design" — the mat is read in passing, once. — [Eagle Mat](https://www.eaglemat.com/blog/?p=373); [CleanLink](https://www.cleanlink.com/news/article/What-To-Consider-When-Creating-Logo-Mats--19367)
- **Brand-protection stance:** some brands "do not allow their logo to be printed on a floor mat for any reason". — [Uline (PDF)](https://img.uline.com/is/content/uline/Custom_Printed_Carpet_Mat_Guide_PDF_ENUS)

### Inferences
- Eight repetitions of a wordmark in one private house — including three bedroom/office balconies seen only by the household — has no precedent in any source, commercial or residential; the residential precedent is one mark at the door of arrival.
- Repetition also multiplies the failure modes documented above: each outdoor printed mark is a separate fade clock, and each small mat forces the thin wordmark toward or below the print minima.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies "how many logo mats is too many"; the recommendation to brand only the primary threshold is an inference from the restraint sources, not a quoted rule.

## Applicability to the Paphos house (synthesis)

**Legibility arithmetic.** At 30 % of mat width the wordmark is 30 cm on the 100-wide mats, 21 cm on the 70-wide mats and 60 cm on the terrace mat. For a tracked six-letter sans, cap height is roughly width ÷ 7 and a *light* weight's stroke roughly 6–8 % of cap height — so ≈ 43 mm caps / ≈ 3 mm strokes on the 100-wide mats, ≈ 30 mm / ≈ 2 mm on the 70-wide mats, ≈ 86 mm / ≈ 6 mm on the terrace (my estimates from the stated proportions; verify against the actual vector). Against the published minima this means: **the two street-side vinyl-mesh mats (100 × 80 and 70 × 60) are the weak point** — the only published vinyl-loop figures demand 9.5–10 mm strokes and 50–76 mm text ([StrandShield](https://personalizeddoormats.com/cdn/shop/files/SPEC_SSVL.pdf); [TMG SpiralLoop](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0565/5216/1447/files/TMG_Design_Guidelines_SpiralLoopInlayMat_EMAIL.pdf)), roughly three times the stroke a light wordmark would have, and printed-coil makers publish no figure at all; expect the printer to invoke the universal "very thin letter forms may need to be made larger or thicker" clause ([Mountville](https://s3.amazonaws.com/aws-website-mountvillecontent-zpj0f/Website-LMI/Product+Design+Specifications/Grand+Impressions+HD.pdf); [TMG](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0565/5216/1447/files/TMG_Design_Guidelines_HogHeavenImpressionsLogoMat_AUS_email.pdf)). If those two doors are to carry the mark at all, it needs a heavier weight (or an outlined/filled redraw) and a larger scale, or a different construction (inlaid coil in a stone tone, or a plain mat). The **printed-nylon mats** (terrace 200 × 100, balconies 100 × 60, the two indoor mats) clear the HD-class minima of 1.5–3.2 mm strokes and 6–13 mm text comfortably, provided the mats are HD dye-injected cut pile (Chromojet / Jet-Print / Grand Impressions class) and **not** printed Berber (7 mm strokes, 40 mm text — the light wordmark fails there). The **dot over the O** is the smallest element: at ≈ 2–3 mm on the 70-wide mats it sits at the dark-colour line minimum (1.5–3.2 mm) and far under the light-colour minimum (6.35 mm) — it survives only because the mark is dark on light; do not invert the scheme on any mat.

**Colour.** Dark green on stone is the favourable polarity (dark on light; the light-colour ¼" rule does not apply), but every maker warns that a light base shows soil first ([Mountville](https://s3.amazonaws.com/aws-website-mountvillecontent-zpj0f/Website-LMI/Product+Design+Specifications/Grand+Impressions+HD.pdf); [TMG](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0565/5216/1447/files/TMG_Design_Guidelines_HogHeavenImpressionsLogoMat_AUS_email.pdf); [First Mats](https://www.firstmats.co.uk/pages/logo-mats)) and StrandShield bans white as a coil base outright — so pitch the base at travertine/sandstone rather than marble-white, especially outdoors. Specify the green as a Pantone **U** reference with the nearest palette chip as fallback ([buyjustrite.eu](https://www.buyjustrite.eu/en/logo-mat-artwork-specifications/)), accept that heat-bonded nylon comes out "a few shades off" and lighter ([Mountville](https://s3.amazonaws.com/aws-website-mountvillecontent-zpj0f/Website-LMI/Product+Design+Specifications/Grand+Impressions+HD.pdf)), and demand a physical strike-off of the exact green-on-stone pair before the run; the house's olive must not drift toward the acid greens of standard mat palettes.

**Sun.** Printed nylon is rated for inside or covered use (blue-wool 5–7; "not intended for use in direct sunlight as fading will occur" — [COBA](https://www.coba.com/product/logomat); [Forbo](https://forbo.blob.core.windows.net/forbodocuments/1767703/Forbo_Coral_Logo_technical_specs_2016.pdf); [Global Industrial](https://www.globalindustrial.com/p/berber-impressions-hd-custom-logo-mat-2-x-3-68-mil-universal-ceated-sbr)). The covered terrace is fine; the three balcony mats at west-facing bedroom/office doors on the Paphos coast are the mats most likely to carry a ghosted wordmark within a season or two — and there is no rental rotation to hide it. Unprinted or solution-dyed mats "stay visually clean longer than any logo mat under direct sun" ([Rugster](https://www.rugster.eu/blogs/news/business-logo-mat-buying-guide)).

**Placement and reading direction.** Centred, single mark, ≥ 1 cm clear of the edge and well inside the maker's imprint area — all satisfied at 30 %. Orientation follows the one rule every maker shares — upright to the person arriving: street/kitchen doors read from outside; the two indoor mats read for the person who has just stepped in (portrait running inward); balcony mats, approached only from the room, read upright from inside.

**How many.** Nothing in the sources supports eight marks; everything that is not a mat salesman's copy points to one — the door of arrival — in the tradition of HAVE at the House of the Faun and the estate name on coir ([99% Invisible](https://99percentinvisible.org/article/ancient-thresholds-classical-welcome-mats-dazzling-medusa-mosaics/); [Make An Entrance](https://www.makeanentrance.com/englishheritage)), with "a clean lobby gets a clean mat" ([Rugster](https://www.rugster.eu/blogs/news/business-logo-mat-buying-guide)) governing the rest. For a museal, Aman/Ando-register house the sourced practice therefore reads: **brand the front-door threshold (ideally the indoor 100 × 110 on marble, where HD print, cover and the arriving gaze all coincide; optionally the terrace), and let the kitchen door, the three balconies and the inner balcony mat be plain, same stone tone, same construction — the family resemblance carried by colour and material, not by repetition of the name.** If the owner wants the street-side mat to speak, a tonal or inlaid mark in a heavier cut is the construction the evidence supports, not a thin printed wordmark on open vinyl coil.
