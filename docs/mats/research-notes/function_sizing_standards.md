# Entrance mats — functional guidance and standards (sizing, zones, safety, outdoor use, maintenance)

Scope note: almost all published numbers come from the *commercial* matting / facility-management world (LEED, WELL, BS 7953, BS 8300, ISSA/CMM trade press, manufacturers). No source found gives a residential standard; the residential "rules" are retailer rules of thumb. Marketing claims are flagged as such. Sources that could not be fetched directly (only seen via search summaries) are marked "(search summary only)".

## 1. Sizing rules — width vs door, depth in steps, outdoor vs indoor, the 2-/3-zone concept

### Takeaway
Commercial guidance converges on "at least as wide as the opening" and a travel-direction length defined by *footfalls* (4–8 steps, i.e. roughly 2–3 m minimum, 6 m+ for busy entrances); the residential rule of thumb is "mat ≥ ~80 % of the door width" with no agreed depth rule. The three-zone concept (exterior scraper → interim → interior absorber) is a commercial framework; in a house it collapses to two zones (an outdoor scraper + an indoor absorber).

### Cited Findings
**Width**
- Residential rule: "Typically, a doormat should be at least 80% percent of the width of your door"; a ~3 ft door → mat ≥ 30 in; standard single-door mat "18 x 30 inches", larger entries "22 x 36 inches"; double doors (~6 ft) → "at least 5 feet wide" — [Canada Mats (retailer blog)](https://canadamats.ca/blogs/blog/how-big-should-your-doormat-be). Other retail guides repeat 75–80 %, some measuring against door-plus-trim or the whole opening where there are sidelights (search summary only) — [Wayfair sizing guide](https://www.wayfair.com/sca/ideas-and-advice/guides/doormat-sizes-T11855); [Homenish](https://www.homenish.com/?p=13684). One retail source inverts the rule ("at least as wide as the door but no more than 80 %") — a contradiction worth noting; the dominant reading treats 80 % as a *minimum* (search summary only).
- WELL Building Standard (AIR concept, entryway systems): mats "at least the width of the entrance and 3 m [10 ft] long in the primary direction of travel" — [WELL v2 feature text](https://standard.wellcertified.com/v8/node/22358); the same wording is quoted in an INTRAsystems RIBA-CPD document — [INTRAsystems CPD (NBS)](https://asset.source.thenbs.com/api/pdf/1709a114-b039-4c21-a184-cf4fcaf04647).
- German trade guidance: minimum depth 1.50 m in the direction of travel and width at least equal to the entrance width (search summary only) — [Baunetz Wissen, Sauberlaufzonen](https://www.baunetzwissen.de/drucken/sauberlaufzonen-50673).
- Manufacturer (GEGGUS): "The matting should span the full width of the door opening, with an extension of at least one meter on each side." — [GEGGUS UK blog](https://geggus-co-uk.netlify.app/company/blog/what-size-should-entrance-matting-be) (manufacturer guidance for commercial entrances).

**Depth / length in steps**
- "They need to get four to five steps on a mat in order for it to work." (Jon Scoles, matting consultant); interior mat "should be four to six feet and cover the length of the doorway"; a "3x5 foot mat" would suffice only if people actually wiped their feet, which "nobody does" — [Cleanlink / Sanitary Maintenance, 2011](https://www.cleanlink.com/sm/article/Proper-Matting-Keeps-Entryways-Clean--13381).
- Hillyard (jan-san distributor): occupants step "once every three feet"; target "between four and eight steps on the entry matting before entering the building"; "For office buildings, the CRI recommends 12 to 15 feet"; "grocery stores or hospitals increases to 20 to 25 feet" — [Hillyard blog](https://www.hillyard.com/blog-230815-indoor-outdoor-entry-walk-off-matting).
- COBA Europe: "recommends allowing a minimum of six footsteps to wipe footwear"; "It takes a minimum of 2.5m length of matting to clean a full revolution of a wheelchair." — [COBA FAQ](https://www.coba.com/faq/size-of-entrance-matting).
- Forbo (Technical Services Manager, blog): industry-standard recommendation of "at least two metres (equates to about 3 or 4 steps) for light use areas" and "up to seven metres for busy use entrances, such as shopping malls, airports"; "nearly 3 metres distance for the wheels to rotate twice" (wheelchair) — [Forbo blog](https://www.forbo.com/flooring/en-uk/inspiration-references/making-spaces-into-places-blog/entrance-flooring-systems-needed-or-just-an-unnecessary-expense/p120az).
- GreenSpec (independent, Peter Mayer / Building LifePlans): "Entrance flooring systems may be 6 - 10 metres in length for effective operation"; "ideally allow for six or more footfalls on the matting" — [GreenSpec](https://greenspec.co.uk/building-design/entrance-mats/).
- GEGGUS: "A minimum mat length of 2.1m in the Walking Direction (Length) is important, to comply with British Standard 7953 (1999)"; recommended 3–5 m normal, 6–7 m heavy, 8–10 m extreme traffic — [GEGGUS UK blog](https://geggus-co-uk.netlify.app/company/blog/what-size-should-entrance-matting-be). **Conflict:** GreenSpec states BS 7953 "does not set out quantifiable criteria by which components can be specified or compared" — [GreenSpec](https://greenspec.co.uk/building-design/entrance-mats/) — so the "2.1 m to comply with BS 7953" figure is a vendor interpretation, not a clause.
- The "15-foot rule" (Notrax/Justrite): 15 ft ensures each foot touches the matting at least 3 times (page returned 403; search summary only) — [Notrax 15 ft rule](https://notrax.justrite.com/15ft-rule); hospitality-supplier version: ~15 ft "lets a guest take about three full strides with each foot" (search summary only) — [National Hospitality Supply blog](https://www.nathosp.com/blog/the-15foot-rule-a-buyers-guide-to-hotel-entrance-matting).
- German occupational-safety leaflet BG BAU A 028 (07/2021): "Bei Gebäudeeingängen Sauberlaufzonen einrichten durch Schmutz- und Feuchtigkeitsaufnehmer. Sauberlaufzonen mindestens 1,5 m lang ausbilden." — [BG BAU A 028 (PDF)](https://www.bgbau.de/fileadmin/Medien-Objekte/Medien/Bausteine/a_028/a_028.pdf).

**Zone concept**
- COBA: Zone 1 (exterior) "Robust barrier matting delivering an effective scraping action to remove the worst of the dirt and grit"; Zone 2 (interim) durable matting removing remaining soil/grit while drying continues; Zone 3 (inside) "secondary internal matting ... finishes the dust removal and drying". No per-zone lengths given — [COBA, How to specify](https://www.coba.com/news/how-to-specify-zone-1549).
- Forbo frames it as Primary Zone (recessed rigid matwell, coarse/ribbed profile, dry soil; external or internal) and Secondary Zone (recessed or surface-mounted, wet and fine particulate soiling); scraper systems for heavy particulate soil, textile systems for wet areas; a two-tier system for mixed soiling — [Forbo blog](https://www.forbo.com/flooring/en-uk/inspiration-references/making-spaces-into-places-blog/entrance-flooring-systems-needed-or-just-an-unnecessary-expense/p120az).
- German "3-Zonen-Reinigung": mats combined to take up "Grob-, Fein- und Feuchtschmutz" (coarse, fine, wet soil) — [Baulinks](https://www.baulinks.de/bodenbelag/sauberlaufzonen-fussmatten.php).
- GreenSpec: for heavy traffic "some manufacturers recommend up to three matting zones", each sized to remove soil, moisture and dust — [GreenSpec](https://greenspec.co.uk/building-design/entrance-mats/).
- Hillyard's two-zone practical version: "Outdoor mats are the first line of defense and is generally designed to scrape larger particles off shoes"; "Indoor mats are designed to capture finer particles to remove residual moisture." — [Hillyard](https://www.hillyard.com/blog-230815-indoor-outdoor-entry-walk-off-matting).

### Inferences
- Using Hillyard's "one step per ~3 ft (≈0.9 m)" pace, a mat 80 cm deep in the travel direction gives roughly one foot-contact; 100–110 cm gives one, occasionally two; 60 cm often gives none unless the person deliberately stops to wipe. The commercial "4–8 steps" / "6 footfalls" targets therefore imply ≥ ~3–5 m of matting, which no household entrance provides; the only commercial figure a house can realistically approach is Forbo's "2 m light-use floor (3–4 steps)", and only by summing the outdoor and indoor mats at the same door (WELL explicitly allows summing indoor + outdoor length).
- The 3-zone system scales down to a house as two zones: outdoor scraper (coarse grit, open structure) + indoor absorber (fine dust, moisture). No source proposes a third zone for a residence.
- For the residential 80 % rule, the reference dimension matters (door leaf vs opening with sidelights); sources disagree, so it is a proportion rule for looks and catchment, not a performance rule.

### Gaps
- No source gives a residential *depth* standard (steps) — only commercial footfall counts.
- No source gives a per-zone length split for the 3-zone system.
- BS 7953:1999 full text not accessible (paywalled); its exact sizing language could not be verified — see Q2.

## 2. Quantified dirt/moisture removal vs mat length; LEED/WELL; BS 7953; manufacturer technical guidance

### Takeaway
The quoted percentages (75–95 % removal at 4.5–6 m, "80 % of soil comes in on shoes") are industry estimates with no cited test method — Forbo's own technical manager concedes no standard test of effectiveness exists. The only codified length requirement is LEED's/WELL's 3 m (10 ft) in the direction of travel, maintained weekly; BS 7953 is descriptive, not quantitative.

### Cited Findings
**Green-building schemes**
- LEED v4 BD+C, EQ credit *Enhanced Indoor Air Quality Strategies*, Option 1 A: "Install permanent entryway systems at least 10 feet (3 meters) long in the primary direction of travel to capture dirt and particulates entering the building at regularly used exterior entrances. Acceptable entryway systems include permanently installed grates, grilles, slotted systems that allow for cleaning underneath, rollout mats, and any other materials manufactured as entryway systems with equivalent or better performance. Maintain all on a weekly basis." — [LEEDuser credit page (NC-v4 EQc1)](https://leeduser.buildinggreen.com/credit/NC-v4/EQc1).
- LEEDuser forum guidance (v4.1 EBOM): "You can put in only one mat of 10 feet centered on the entrance"; "permanent entryway systems at every regularly used exterior entrances ... at least 10 feet long" — [LEEDuser forum](https://leeduser.buildinggreen.com/node/65997). A GBCI reply quoted on LEEDuser says the system may be "fully or partially outdoors, as long as it is continuous and at least 10 feet (3 meters) long in the primary direction of travel", and the 10 ft rule is "strict" (search summary only) — [LEEDuser forum, walk mat size](https://leeduser.buildinggreen.com/forum/walk-mat-size).
- WELL v2: entryway system "at least the width of the entrance and 3 m [10 ft] long in the primary direction of travel", length counted as the sum of indoor and outdoor length — [WELL v2](https://standard.wellcertified.com/v8/node/22358).
- The INTRAsystems CPD claims "Entrance Matting is not directly eligible for BREEAM points or LEED credits" — [INTRAsystems CPD](https://asset.source.thenbs.com/api/pdf/1709a114-b039-4c21-a184-cf4fcaf04647) — contradicted by the LEED credit text above (entryway systems are one of the credit's Option 1 strategies); treat the CPD statement as loose.

**Percent-removal figures (all unattributed estimates)**
- "ISSA estimates 80 percent of the soil, dust and contaminants within a facility come in through the door" (incoming load, not removal); "it costs at least $500 to remove a pound of soil tracked into a facility"; 12–15 ft of matting inside and outside "to capture 80 to 90 percent of the dirt on people's shoes" (Scoles) — [Cleanlink](https://www.cleanlink.com/sm/article/Proper-Matting-Keeps-Entryways-Clean--13381).
- A BNP continuing-education course credits ISSA with "fifteen feet of effective, high-performance matting can trap and hold as much as 75 percent of this soil at the door; 30 feet can remove as much as 100 percent" (search summary only) — [BNP CE course](https://continuingeducation.bnpmedia.com/courses/turtle-plastics/entrance-mats-keep-it-clean/2/).
- Hillyard: "some studies estimate that occupants track in 85% of the dirt that enters a building"; "a twenty-foot entry mat can capture over 90% of the dirt" — [Hillyard](https://www.hillyard.com/blog-230815-indoor-outdoor-entry-walk-off-matting).
- Forbo (manufacturer): systems with a 6 m walking length "have been proven to stop up to 95% of all types of incoming soiling"; cleaning reduced "in some cases by as much as 65%"; "up to 75% of the total flooring soiling can be held and controlled in small defined matting areas" — no study named; the same author states there is "no test method to measure the effectiveness" of entrance flooring — [Forbo blog](https://www.forbo.com/flooring/en-uk/inspiration-references/making-spaces-into-places-blog/entrance-flooring-systems-needed-or-just-an-unnecessary-expense/p120az). **Marketing claim.**
- German trade figure: "70 bis 80% aller Verschmutzungen werden von außen mit den Füßen in das Gebäude getragen", ~7 g of soil per visitor — [Baulinks](https://www.baulinks.de/bodenbelag/sauberlaufzonen-fussmatten.php).

**BS 7953 and BS 8300**
- BS 7953:1999 "Entrance flooring systems — Selection, installation and maintenance", status current/under review; scope: describes interior and exterior systems and gives recommendations for selection, planning, installation and maintenance — [BSI Knowledge](https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/products/entrance-flooring-systems-selection-installation-and-maintenance).
- Per Forbo, BS 7953 §3.1 says a system "should scrape, wipe and retain soil" and should achieve "the maximum number of footfalls within the available space"; variants listed: grille, random looped, ribbed, modular, textile — [Forbo blog](https://www.forbo.com/flooring/en-uk/inspiration-references/making-spaces-into-places-blog/entrance-flooring-systems-needed-or-just-an-unnecessary-expense/p120az).
- GreenSpec: BS 7953 "does not set out quantifiable criteria by which components can be specified or compared"; manufacturer data "inconsistent and not always based on British or European standards" — [GreenSpec](https://greenspec.co.uk/building-design/entrance-mats/).
- BS 8300-2:2018 (as summarised in the INTRAsystems CPD): an entrance flooring system should remove water and debris from foot and wheeled traffic "in commercial buildings and buildings used by general public", sized for traffic and for "the circumference of a large wheelchair wheel (a minimum of 2000mm)" — [INTRAsystems CPD](https://asset.source.thenbs.com/api/pdf/1709a114-b039-4c21-a184-cf4fcaf04647).

### Inferences
- Every percentage in circulation traces to consultant estimates or manufacturer copy; none to a published, repeatable test. The honest reading is qualitative: removal rises with the number of foot contacts, and roughly 3 m is where codified schemes (LEED/WELL) draw the line.
- LEED's acceptance of roll-out mats *if maintained weekly* is the one place where a loose-laid mat is explicitly sanctioned by a standard — conditional on maintenance, not on construction.

### Gaps
- BS 7953:1999 body text (paywalled) — could not confirm whether it contains any length figure (the 2.1 m and 6 m figures attributed to it are vendor statements).
- No primary ISSA document found for the 80 %/75 %/$500 figures; all are second-hand via trade press.
- No emco, Kleen-Tex or Milliken technical white paper with quantified removal-vs-length data was located.

## 3. Safety — edge heights/bevels, trip hazards of loose-laid and borderless mats, slip resistance on polished stone, thickness vs door clearance, curling

### Takeaway
Accessibility codes treat any edge above ~4–6 mm as a change in level that needs a bevel (ADA: ≤¼ in vertical, ¼–½ in beveled 1:2; BG BAU: >4 mm is a Stolperstelle, bevel ≤25° up to 2 cm), and UK guidance wants surface-laid mats rubber-backed, chamfer-edged and securely fixed; a 7–9 mm borderless mat therefore presents a vertical edge that every code would bevel. Slip classes for the mat itself (DIN 51130 R-class, EN 13893 DS, pendulum PTV ≥ 36) exist but must be requested from the supplier; entrance floors are typically R9 indoors / R10–R11 outdoors.

### Cited Findings
**Edge height / changes in level**
- ADA Standards §302.2 (carpet): maximum pile height "1/2″ measured to the backing, cushion, or pad"; "Exposed edges must have trim on the entire length of the exposed edge and be fastened to the floor to prevent curling"; "Carpeting must be securely attached so that it does not shift or buckle against wheeled traffic". §303: "Changes in level can be up to ¼″ without treatment, or ½″ if beveled with a slope no steeper than 1:2"; "Changes in level above a ½″ must be treated as a ramp or curb ramp" — [US Access Board, Chapter 3 guide](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/guides/chapter-3-floor-and-ground-surfaces).
- California accessibility guide note: the chapter's requirements "would not apply to the mats themselves", but ADA regulations "can provide helpful guidance in ensuring that mats do not constitute barriers"; movable mats that impede access "may need to be moved or removed" (search summary only) — [Corada, CBC 11B-302.2 commentary](https://www.corada.com/documents/2025CBCPG/11b-302-2-carpet).
- Germany, BG BAU A 028 (07/2021): "Stolperstellen sind in Räumen definiert als Höhenunterschiede von mehr als 4 mm unter ebenen Bedingungen"; floor coverings include "Auflagen wie Matten, Roste, Abdeckvliese oder Teppiche"; strips/covers in walkways to be laid "kipp- und trittsicher, bündig, höhengleich mit der Fußbodenoberfläche ... und im Fußboden verankern"; "Bei Höhenunterschieden bis zu 2 cm eine Abschrägung mit einem Winkel von höchstens 25° anbringen. Größere Höhenunterschiede durch begehbare Schrägrampen überbrücken."; "Fußbodenauflagen dürfen keine Stolperstellen bilden."; "Sauberlaufzonen gegen Verrutschen sichern und bündig mit dem Bodenbelag abschließen." — [BG BAU A 028 (PDF)](https://www.bgbau.de/fileadmin/Medien-Objekte/Medien/Bausteine/a_028/a_028.pdf).
- UK, BS 8300-2:2018 as summarised: "Matting should either have its surface level with the adjacent floor finish or, if surface laid, be of a type that has a rubber backing and chamfered edges. This should be securely fixed to the floor to avoid the risk of slips and trips. Deep pile carpets and coir matting should not be used on the surface of the floor or within a mat well." — [INTRAsystems CPD (NBS)](https://asset.source.thenbs.com/api/pdf/1709a114-b039-4c21-a184-cf4fcaf04647).
- UK HSE (workplace floors): "Ensure they are securely fixed and have no curling edges"; "Select suitable floor coverings, taking into account expected wear and tear"; "Provide doormats, foot-scrapers etc to prevent water or mud being walked onto dry floors" — [HSE, slips and trips](https://www.hse.gov.uk/Waste/slipsandtrips.htm).
- RIBA Access Audit checklist for entrances asks whether matting is "of a suitable, non-compressible material", "firmly fixed (loose-laid mats avoided)" and "flush with adjacent floor finishes" (PDF now 404; search summary only) — [RIBA access audit – Entrances](https://www.architecture.com/-/media/GatherContent/Business-Benchmarking/Additional-Documents/9-Entrancespdf.pdf).
- Vendor-stated tolerances circulate — "flush within 6 mm" for recessed mats, "maximum 13 mm raised edge" for surface-mounted, "max 6 mm upstand at any threshold", "bevelled edges, max 12 mm" — and are mutually inconsistent and unverified against any standard (search summary only) — [Rubberco buyer's guide](https://rubberco.co.uk/blogs/news/entrance-matting-uk-complete-2026-commercial-buyers-guide-types-bs-7953-dda-sizing). **Vendor claims.**

**Slip resistance**
- DIN 51130 ramp test (shod, oil): R9 ≈ 6–10°, R10 > 10–19°, R11 > 19–27°; recommended R9 for indoor entrance areas and "R11 or R10 V4" for outdoor entrance areas (search summary only) — [Villeroy & Boch technical info](https://villeroy-boch-tiles.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/VuB_Technische-Informationen_01_Trittsicherheit-Rutschhemmung_2026-01-01_EN.pdf). The same values appear in the German workplace rule ASR A1.5/1,2 Annex 2 as reproduced by BG BAU: "0.1 Eingangsbereiche, innen R9 / 0.2 Eingangsbereiche, außen R 11 oder R 10 V 4" — [BG BAU A 028](https://www.bgbau.de/fileadmin/Medien-Objekte/Medien/Bausteine/a_028/a_028.pdf).
- Status: DIN 51130 is carried into DIN EN 16165 Annex B ("the test method, the measuring principle and the test materials (test shoe, motor oil) are the same"); EN 16165 reports "only" the slip angle, a national informative annex keeps the R-classes; the pendulum is Annex C; EN 16165 "does not deal with requirements for floorings"; EN 13893's "Mix-Slider" was the slider "used in EN 13893 for testing in the context of placing on the market" — [DGUV IFA FAQ on DIN EN 16165](https://dguv.de/ifa/pruefung-zertifizierung/pruefung-von-bodenbelaegen/faq-zur-din-en-16165/index-2.jsp).
- EN 13893:2002 "Resilient, laminate and textile floor coverings — Measurement of dynamic coefficient of friction on dry floor surfaces" (current) — [BSI](https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/products/resilient-laminate-and-textile-floor-coverings-measurement-of-dynamic-coefficient-of-friction-on-dry-floor-surfaces); under EN 14041 (CE marking) a covering for dry conditions with dynamic coefficient > 0.30 carries the "DS" symbol, and textile pile carpets generally meet DS — [carpetyourlife.com](https://carpetyourlife.com/en/about-carpet/prodis/fcss/additional-characteristics).
- Pendulum (UKSRG/HSE): PTV 0–24 high slip potential, 25–35 moderate, 36+ low (search summary) — [HSE guideline table via Johnson Tiles](https://www.johnson-tiles.com/media/pdf/JT_Website_-_Download_HSE_Slip_Resistance_Guidelines_-_April_2021.pdf); Irish HSA: PTV 36 ≈ "1 in 1 million probability of a slip", recommends wet PTV 36+ in final use conditions — [HSA Ireland](https://hsa.ie/topics/slips_trips_falls/pedestrian_surfaces/measuring_slip_resistance/). A Forbo statement lists moderate as 24–35 (boundary discrepancy) — [Forbo slip statement](https://forbo.blob.core.windows.net/forbodocuments/657022/Forbo%20Flooring%20Statement%20on%20Slip%20Resistance%20Testing%20November%202019.pdf).
- Example of how mats are rated: a rubber entrance mat datasheet quotes "DIN 51130:2014 R12" (search summary only) — [Rubber Company datasheet](https://therubbercompany.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/R-CLEAN-CS-R-RubbaClean-R-Entrance-Matting-Datasheet-REV-2.0.pdf).

**Thickness vs door clearance; curling**
- Door gaps "typically range from 0.5 to 0.75 inches", and a mat "needs to be thinner than this measurement by at least 0.1 to 0.2 inches to allow for free movement"; a too-thick mat "will catch on the door as it swings inward" (search summary only; retail guide) — [EngineerFix thin-mat guide](https://engineerfix.com/what-to-look-for-in-a-thin-door-mat/).
- Undercut vs clearance: undercut = frame bottom to door bottom; clearance = door bottom to finished floor; with ½ in carpet a ¾ in undercut leaves ~¼ in clearance (search summary only) — [FCIA, Measuring door gaps](https://www.fcia.org/Portals/0/180324%20Measuring%20Door%20Gaps.pdf).
- GreenSpec lists typical system thicknesses: 13 mm washable internal mat, 16 mm heavy-traffic PVC mat, 15 mm PVC tile grid, 18 mm aluminium grid — [GreenSpec](https://greenspec.co.uk/building-design/entrance-mats/).
- Curling: replace mats "that are curled, ripped, or worn" and "secure edges with carpet tape if needed" (healthcare safety course; search summary only) — [OSHAcademy](https://www.oshacademy.com/courses/training/624-healthcare-slip-trip-fall-prevention/624-1-13.php).

### Inferences
- A 7 mm or 9 mm mat with a square (borderless) edge is above the ADA ¼ in (6.4 mm) vertical allowance and far above the German 4 mm trip-step definition; both frameworks would call for a bevel. The standard 2.5 cm rubber perimeter on loop/nylon mats is exactly that bevel (a 1:2 slope over 9 mm needs ≥ 18 mm run; 2.5 cm satisfies it). Removing the border trades a code-style transition for a vertical lip. None of this is *legally* binding in a private residence, but the geometry that trips a foot is identical.
- Loose-laid is tolerated by LEED (roll-out mats, weekly maintenance) but discouraged by every accessibility/safety source (BS 8300 summary, BG BAU, HSE, RIBA audit) unless "securely fixed". For a house, the practical proxy for "fixed" is a non-slip backing on a clean, dry floor plus visual checks for curl.
- On polished marble/travertine the governing slip question is the mat's *backing on the stone* (migration) more than the mat surface; no source gives a backing-on-stone test standard — PVC/nitrile backings are described qualitatively as anti-slip. Ask the supplier for a DIN 51130 / EN 16165 slip angle (or EN 13893 DS) for the surface and a statement on backing grip on polished stone.

### Gaps
- No standard found that sets a slip-resistance class for the *underside* (backing-to-floor) of a loose mat.
- DIN 18040-1 text on Schmutzfangmatten not located; sources only confirm the general threshold-free entrance principle and vendor "rollstuhlgeeignet" claims (search summary) — [weka DIN 18040-1 summary](https://www.weka.de/architekten-ingenieure/din-18040-1/).
- Approved Document M's own matting paragraph could not be fetched; the BS 8300-2:2018 wording above comes via a manufacturer CPD and should be checked against the standard.

## 4. Outdoor exposure — open vs backed mats, trapped water, sun fading of printed nylon, printed "indoor" mats on covered terraces

### Takeaway
Manufacturers of printed (dye-sublimated/jet-printed) nylon mats limit them to indoor or *covered* outdoor use out of direct sun because printed colour fades; solution-dyed nylon or UV-stabilised vinyl is what is sold for sun. Closed PVC/rubber backings keep water under the mat (mildew/odour; staining risk on porous stone), so outdoor mats are either open-structure/drained or lifted regularly to dry.

### Cited Findings
- Printed-mat maker (SnugZ, via distributor): mats are for "indoor or outdoor covered use" and "not intended for use in direct sunlight as fading will occur" (search summary only) — [mpowerpromo product page](https://store.mpowerpromo.com/products/lmbbi46-snugz). Another printed mat listing: "indoor/outdoor use in covered areas. Not UV resistant" (search summary only; page not individually verified) — [mpowerpromo logomats](https://breakercolab.mpowerpromo.com/products/3559-46-logomats).
- Kleen-Tex "wash+dry" printed mats: nylon pile, nitrile rubber backing, printed surface; care: vacuum, "Machine wash up to 60°C", "Tumble dry on medium temperature"; the technical data sheet adds: wash separately, max 60 °C, mild detergent, spin ~800 rpm, "Lay the mat flat to dry or use a tumble dryer" — [Kleen-Tex wash+dry](https://kleen-tex.com/products/wash-dry); [wash+dry technical data sheet](https://www.expertdecor.fr/media/pdf/c9/c0/70/Technical-Data-Sheet_w-d_Decor.pdf). The line is marketed as indoor; no statement on sun exposure found (search summary).
- Outdoor-rated logo mats use "Solution Dyed fibers for 100% UV-resistance" (phs, search summary only) — [phs outdoor premium logo mat overview (PDF)](https://www.phs.co.uk/media/3887/outdoor-premium-logo-mat-overview.pdf).
- Vinyl-mesh (loop) printed mats: "perfect for wet, exterior areas where traction and scraping are necessary"; "1/2" thick extruded vinyl"; UV stabilizers "help these logo mats resist fading, even when placed outside or in direct sunlight"; offered "with either a solid backing and black edging" — [American Floor Mats](http://www.americanfloormats.com/vinyl-mesh-printed-logo-mats/). (Note: a *solid* backing on a vinyl-loop mat removes the drain-through advantage of the open structure.)
- Trapped water: "An outdoor mat that traps water instead of shedding it creates a breeding ground for mildew, slime, and odor"; thick absorbent layers "take a long time to dry out" (UK retailer guide; search summary only) — [Mats4U guide](https://mats4u.co.uk/blogs/news/the-ultimate-guide-to-weather-resistant-mats-in-the-uk-a-deep-dive-into-durability-and-performance). Rubber-backed outdoor mats sold with drainage holes/grooves or underside studs so water "drains away" (product listings; search summary only) — [Bob Vila, best outdoor doormats](https://www.bobvila.com/articles/best-outdoor-doormats.md).
- Stone staining: a stone-care authority warns against "rubber or jute-backed mats/rugs as they themselves may stain the stone surface" (search summary only) — [Singapore BCA stone guide (PDF)](https://bca.gov.sg/publications/EnhancementSeries/others/stone8.pdf); a stone-restoration FAQ advises staying away from rubber-backed mats on marble because moisture gets under them and may stain (search summary only) — [Stone Restoration manual FAQ](https://sr-manual.com/kb/article/what-do-i-tell-my-customers-when-they-ask-what-type-of-floor-mats-do-i-recommend-for-use-on-a-marble-floor-i-was-told-to-stay-away-from-rubber-backed-mats-600.html). Wet leaves left on travertine "overnight—or longer" release tannins that penetrate the stone, "particularly on shaded patios" (search summary only) — [Outercle travertine stain removal](https://www.outercle.com/blogs/outercle-education/travertine-stain-removal).
- German planning advice: where rain/snow are expected, plan a Schmutzfangwanne (drained mat tray) under the entrance mat — [Baulinks](https://www.baulinks.de/bodenbelag/sauberlaufzonen-fussmatten.php).
- Forbo: primary (external) zone = recessed rigid matwell with coarse/ribbed profile; textile systems are for wet/fine soil *inside* — [Forbo blog](https://www.forbo.com/flooring/en-uk/inspiration-references/making-spaces-into-places-blog/entrance-flooring-systems-needed-or-just-an-unnecessary-expense/p120az).

### Inferences
- A covered terrace is not "indoor": the covering removes rain but not low-angle sun, wind-blown dust, salt air or humidity. A *west*-facing covered terrace in Cyprus receives direct late-afternoon sun under the overhang for much of the year — exactly the "direct sunlight" case the printed-mat makers exclude; expect progressive fading of printed colour (the catalogue's own "not for extensive sunlight" is consistent with this).
- A PVC-backed mat on exterior travertine is effectively a closed membrane: wind-driven rain, hosing or condensation that gets under it stays there. The documented consequences are mildew/odour under the mat and, on porous stone, discolouration/efflorescence rings matching the mat footprint. Open-structure (drain-through, unbacked) vinyl loop or a drained tray is what the outdoor zone literature assumes.

### Gaps
- No manufacturer statement found that *explicitly* addresses the covered-terrace case for printed nylon ("covered outdoor" is permitted by some makers, "no direct sun" by all); no published fading-rate data (one blog's "ghostly pale in under ninety days" is anecdotal — not cited as fact).
- No authoritative test on PVC/nitrile backings staining travertine specifically; sources are stone-care guidance for marble.

## 5. Maintenance guidance (shake/vacuum/wash frequency, lifting to dry)

### Takeaway
Commercial baselines: vacuum daily, wet-clean/extract weekly to monthly, LEED requires at least weekly maintenance; washable nylon/nitrile mats are machine-washed at ≤ 60 °C and dried flat; outdoor mats are shaken, hosed and hung to dry, and all backed mats should be lifted so both mat and floor dry.

### Cited Findings
- LEED v4: "Maintain all on a weekly basis" (entryway systems) — [LEEDuser NC-v4 EQc1](https://leeduser.buildinggreen.com/credit/NC-v4/EQc1).
- Hillyard: vacuuming "In most situations, once daily is sufficient"; extraction for clean-in-place mats "monthly extraction may be all that is needed", more often "in damp climates or during the winter"; an air mover "recommended to reduce drying time" — [Hillyard](https://www.hillyard.com/blog-230815-indoor-outdoor-entry-walk-off-matting).
- GreenSpec: daily vacuum and soil removal; weekly water-and-detergent clean; periodic extraction; "Typical service lives are in the range of 3 – 10 years" (decorative nylon internal mat 3–5 yr) — [GreenSpec](https://greenspec.co.uk/building-design/entrance-mats/).
- Kleen-Tex wash+dry: vacuum; machine wash ≤ 60 °C, mild detergent, low spin (~800 rpm); lay flat or tumble dry medium — [Kleen-Tex](https://kleen-tex.com/products/wash-dry); [data sheet](https://www.expertdecor.fr/media/pdf/c9/c0/70/Technical-Data-Sheet_w-d_Decor.pdf).
- Turtle Mat (Kleen-Tex group) outdoor mats: shake off loose dirt, hose down, hang to dry (search summary only) — [Turtle Mat washing guide](https://www.turtlemat.co.uk/pages/washing-guide).
- Generic care: "hang mat to dry and make sure it is fully dry before putting it back" (product care notes; search summary only) — [Bob Vila outdoor doormats](https://www.bobvila.com/articles/best-outdoor-doormats.md).
- Cleanlink: sweep sidewalks "at least once a week"; "matting should be used 12 months a year" — [Cleanlink](https://www.cleanlink.com/sm/article/Proper-Matting-Keeps-Entryways-Clean--13381).
- HSE: "Keep them in a good state of repair" — [HSE](https://www.hse.gov.uk/Waste/slipsandtrips.htm).

### Inferences
- For a residence the commercial frequencies scale to: shake/vacuum the indoor mats weekly (daily in rainy weeks), machine-wash the nylon mats a few times a year or when visibly soiled (≤ 60 °C, dry flat — never on a radiator), hose the outdoor vinyl mats and, crucially, lift *every* backed outdoor/terrace mat after rain or hosing so the stone beneath dries; inspect for edge curl at the same time.

### Gaps
- No source gives a residential wash/lift frequency; all frequencies are facility-management baselines.

## 6. Applicability to the Paphos house (eight loose-laid mats, travertine / anthracite stone / marble)

### Takeaway
The commercial length rules cannot be met at a house and do not need to be; what carries over is the two-zone logic, the edge/bevel physics, the slip and backing questions on polished stone, and the manufacturers' own exclusion of printed nylon from direct sun.

### Cited Findings
- Reference figures used below: 80 % of door width (residential) — [Canada Mats](https://canadamats.ca/blogs/blog/how-big-should-your-doormat-be); one step ≈ 0.9 m, 4–8 steps — [Hillyard](https://www.hillyard.com/blog-230815-indoor-outdoor-entry-walk-off-matting); 2 m ≈ 3–4 steps for light use — [Forbo](https://www.forbo.com/flooring/en-uk/inspiration-references/making-spaces-into-places-blog/entrance-flooring-systems-needed-or-just-an-unnecessary-expense/p120az); indoor+outdoor length may be summed, width ≥ entrance — [WELL](https://standard.wellcertified.com/v8/node/22358); ≤ 6.4 mm vertical / 6.4–13 mm beveled 1:2 — [Access Board](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/guides/chapter-3-floor-and-ground-surfaces); > 4 mm = Stolperstelle, bevel ≤ 25° — [BG BAU A 028](https://www.bgbau.de/fileadmin/Medien-Objekte/Medien/Bausteine/a_028/a_028.pdf); surface-laid mats rubber-backed, chamfered, securely fixed; no coir/deep pile — [BS 8300-2 via INTRAsystems](https://asset.source.thenbs.com/api/pdf/1709a114-b039-4c21-a184-cf4fcaf04647); printed mats "not intended for use in direct sunlight" — [SnugZ via mpowerpromo](https://store.mpowerpromo.com/products/lmbbi46-snugz); rubber-backed mats may stain stone — [BCA stone guide](https://bca.gov.sg/publications/EnhancementSeries/others/stone8.pdf).

### Inferences
- **Front door (outdoor 100×80 vinyl mesh on travertine + indoor 100×110 nylon on marble):** this is the house's only true two-zone entrance. Combined travel-direction depth ≈ 0.8 + 1.1 = 1.9 m — just under Forbo's 2 m light-use floor (3–4 steps), which is as good as a residence gets; the 10 ft LEED/WELL rule is irrelevant here. Check the 100 cm width against the actual door-plus-reveal (≥ 80 % rule; if the opening is wider than ~125 cm the mat reads narrow). Orientation matters: lay the long axis *across* the door so the 80/110 cm are in the direction of travel.
- **Kitchen side door (70×60 outdoor vinyl on dark stone) and the 70×60 indoor balcony mat:** 60 cm in the travel direction is below one stride; these function as wipe-stop mats, not walk-off mats — acceptable for secondary doors, but they only work if used deliberately.
- **Terrace 200×100 and balcony 3× 100×60 (printed nylon, 7 mm, PVC backing, catalogued "indoor, not for extensive sunlight"):** the covered west-facing terrace is the case the printed-mat makers exclude; expect fading on the sun-exposed strip and treat the mats as consumables, or ask the supplier for a solution-dyed / UV-stabilised alternative for those four positions. A closed PVC backing on exterior travertine should be lifted after rain/hosing (mildew and stone discolouration under the footprint); if the mats are to stay down, an open-structure or drained product is what the outdoor literature assumes. Nothing in the functional literature supports an *interior-grade* nylon mat as a permanent exterior fixture.
- **Borderless (no 2.5 cm perimeter) on 7–9 mm mats:** every code quoted would bevel an edge of that height; the border *is* the bevel. Borderless is defensible aesthetically on the indoor marble mats (7 mm, low traffic, no wheeled traffic, family users) but is the weakest choice at the street-side front door (visitors, night arrivals, possible wheeled luggage) and on the terrace sliding-door threshold where people step through glass doors looking outward. If borderless is kept, specify a factory-sealed, heat-cut or hot-knife edge, keep the backing fully non-slip, and inspect for curl — curl is what turns a 9 mm edge into a trip.
- **Slip on polished stone:** request from the Cypriot supplier the DIN 51130 / EN 16165 slip angle or EN 13893 DS declaration for the mat surfaces (R9/DS is the realistic indoor benchmark; R10–R11 for the two exterior vinyl mats) and a statement on backing grip on *polished* travertine and marble; no standard covers the backing-to-stone interface, so a short wet trial on the actual stone is the honest test.
- **Door clearance:** only the hinged doors matter (front door, kitchen door); confirm clearance under the leaf ≥ mat thickness + ~3–5 mm (9 mm outside at the front door if the mat sits under the swing, 7 mm inside). Sliding doors on terrace and balconies do not sweep over the mats.
- **Maintenance:** weekly shake/vacuum indoors, machine-wash nylon mats ≤ 60 °C and dry flat, hose the vinyl mats, lift every backed mat on stone to dry — and at the kitchen door on dark anthracite stone, watch for lime/efflorescence rings under a closed backing.

### Gaps
- Door-leaf widths and the terrace door opening width were not given, so the 80 % check is left as a rule to apply, not a result.
- Supplier's actual slip-test data and UV/colour-fastness data for the printed nylon line are unknown; the catalogue note is the only evidence.
