# Entrance mats on natural stone and sealed concrete — backing compatibility, moisture, abrasion, sealers, maker positions, alternatives

Scope note on evidence quality. The *mechanisms* of backing-induced discolouration are well documented by the resilient-flooring (vinyl/linoleum) industry and by wood-floor literature; for **marble, travertine and limestone the documentary base is thin** — it consists of (i) short, unexplained cautions in stone-industry and stone-restoration guidance, (ii) rug-trade claims about porous stone, and (iii) a few forum/anecdotal cases, none of them on polished marble specifically. For **sealed concrete** there is one strong primary source (a sealer manufacturer's troubleshooting guide). Each finding below says which material it was observed on. Where a source could only be read through a search excerpt (the page itself was blocked), it is marked "(excerpt only)".

## 1. Staining / yellowing / discolouration of stone and sealed concrete by rubber or PVC backings — which mechanisms, documented on what, which backings are considered safe

### Takeaway
Two distinct chemistries are documented: **antioxidant staining** (rubber/latex antioxidants react with the floor finish → irreversible yellowing; named and described by the vinyl industry) and **plasticizer migration** (softeners leaching out of flexible PVC/rubber into finishes, grout or porous substrates). Both are firmly documented on vinyl, wood and sealed/painted concrete; for marble/travertine/limestone the stone trade repeats the warning ("rubber- or jute-backed mats may stain the stone") without published case studies, and rug retailers add that calcareous stones "readily absorb" migrating oils. Backings consistently described as safer are felt, natural rubber labelled non-staining, polyurethane, "vinyl-backed non-staining" (in the vinyl trade), and anything explicitly labelled plasticizer-free/phthalate-free; backings consistently flagged are SBR/synthetic-latex rubber with clay fillers, unlabelled rubber, and PVC on finishes it can soften.

### Cited Findings
- Armstrong's guidance, relayed by the Philadelphia Inquirer's Alan J. Heavens: rubber contains antioxidants to keep it from becoming brittle; "Some antioxidants react chemically with ingredients in flexible vinyl to produce a yellowish stain"; the vinyl industry calls this "antioxidant staining"; "Any rubber containing these antioxidants in contact with the floor could produce this staining"; "the experts say it is highly unlikely that the yellow can be removed"; the remedy is to ensure mats are of the "nonstaining" type of rubber — [Inquirer / Armstrong](https://www.inquirer.com/philly/business/real_estate/diy/20101119_Your_Place__Rubber_mats_may_turn_vinyl_flooring_yellow.html)
- A flooring distributor (Jacobsen, NZ): rugs are fine "as long as they are labelled 'colourfast'"; "We recommend non-staining, vinyl-backed mats or woven rugs"; "We do not recommend the use of rubber- or latex-backed mats because the chemical (antioxidant) used to keep the backing from becoming brittle can permanently stain your floor" (vinyl) — [Jacobsen](https://connect.jacobsen.co.nz/knowledge/can-i-use-rugs-or-mats-on-my-vinyl-floor)
- Same mechanism, vinyl/linoleum: non-skid rubber backings contain antioxidants that react with the floor and produce "a characteristic staining or discoloration" which "is not removable"; use only mats "clearly marked as non-staining to vinyl floors" — [Natural Handyman](https://www.naturalhandyman.com/iip/inffloor/inflino6.html)
- Floor & Decor (vinyl): neither natural nor synthetic rubber nor latex-backed rugs should be used on any vinyl, because they react with the top layers and cause discoloration (excerpt only) — [Floor & Decor](https://flooranddecor.com/help-center/product-questions/help-product-vinyl/hc-can-i-use-rubber-backed-rugs-on-my-vinyl-floors.html)
- **Sealed concrete, primary source.** Euclid Chemical's *Sealer Troubleshooting Guide*, Q "Why are carpets, mats, or garage door weatherstripping sticking to sealed concrete?": "Cause: Rubber additives in mats, carpet backing, or weatherstripping may chemically react with concrete sealers, resulting in the sealer becoming sticky. Prevention: Do not place rubber backed mats or carpet on sealed concrete. Do not seal area of concrete where rubber items will be in contact. Solution: Remove sources of rubber/sealer direct contact." The same guide: "Most concrete sealers will not prevent stains"; a sealer "turned white" when applied heavily and "moisture trapped underneath the sealer has caused it to lose adhesion"; sealer bubbles when applied "in hot weather/direct sun" — [Euclid Chemical](https://www.buildsite.com/pdf/euclid/REZ-SEAL-Technical-Notes-2150933.pdf)
- **Painted/sealed concrete outdoors (anecdotal case):** a rubber floor-protector pad under a rug on a Florida lanai stuck to the painted, sealed floor; rubbing alcohol used for removal "took off sealer"; one commenter ended up replacing the floor — [Hometalk thread](https://www.hometalk.com/32240680/diy/grow)
- **Stone, trade caution (excerpt only):** Singapore BCA good-practice guide on stone: caution should be taken on the use of rubber or jute-backed mats/rugs "as they themselves may stain the stone surface" — [BCA Singapore, stone8.pdf](https://bca.gov.sg/publications/EnhancementSeries/others/stone8.pdf)
- **Marble, restoration-trade Q&A (excerpt only; the article is now access-restricted in the Stone Forensics / Surface Care PROS knowledge base):** rubber-backed mats, and some jute-backed mats, can stain a marble floor; recommend cloth-backed floor mats and lifting mats at least once a week so moisture does not build up underneath — [Surface Care PROS KB](https://sr-manual.com/kb/article/what-do-i-tell-my-customers-when-they-ask-what-type-of-floor-mats-do-i-recommend-for-use-on-a-marble-floor-i-was-told-to-stay-away-from-rubber-backed-mats-600.html)
- **Marble, cleaning-trade guide (excerpt only):** rubber under rugs is a problem on marble "because of its sulfur content" — [Baneclene marble care](https://www.baneclene.com/homeowners/Content.aspx?xps=NTU2)
- **Calcareous stone, rug trade:** "Marble, travertine, and limestone will readily absorb chemicals"; "Synthetic pads used for the liner will leave oily residue which will penetrate porous stone"; low-grade plastic/rubber pads cause "plasticizer migration … a permanent yellow stain that occurs on wide-plank white oak and marble" — [Ren Collection (rug retailer)](https://rencollection.com/blogs/news/best-rug-pads-in-dallas-tx-protect-your-floors-and-rugs)
- Rug-pad maker on marble: synthetic latex pads with clay fillers "break down prematurely, potentially staining your valuable marble floors"; avoid "synthetic adhesives and glues which can stick to and stain floors"; natural rubber (alone or with felt) recommended — [RugPadUSA](https://www.rugpadusa.com/articles/protect-and-preserve-your-marble-floors-with-these-rug-pads)
- Vendor (polyurethane mats) summarising the PVC side: "The plasticizers in these materials migrate into floor finishes over time"; PVC leaches "phthalates and softening agents"; rubber and PVC compounds "seep into grout lines and cause staining that is nearly impossible to remove"; on polished porcelain "chemical residue from the mat can dull the surface finish in the exact outline of the mat"; "temperature fluctuations" and repeated compression accelerate migration; natural stone is not mentioned — [WellnessMats (vendor blog)](https://www.wellnessmats.com/blogs/the-wellness-journal/rubber-backed-mats-damage-floors)
- German explainer (parquet; secondary): phthalate plasticizers are not chemically bound and migrate "mit der Zeit" into the surface coating; antioxidants/anti-ageing additives react with the finish and under UV through windows give dark or yellow spots; rubber is air- and water-impermeable so moisture cannot escape; underfloor heating accelerates the processes ("die Weichmacher gasen schneller aus"); safe backings named: felt, jute, true natural latex without harmful plasticizers, products labelled "weichmacherfrei" — [antwort.net](https://www.antwort.net/a/antwort.net/warum-fuehren-manche-gummiruecken-von-fussmatten-zu-dauerhaften-verfaerbungen-auf-parkettboeden.html)
- **Stone case (anecdotal, slate):** a stone-care professionals' list post: a rubber mat on an indoor rough slate floor left rubber residue; after scraping, "stains all over the floor" remained; a reply suggested scraping heavy residue and possibly a solvent such as MEK — [Stone & Tile PROS list, question](https://stoneandtilepros.simplelists.com/sccpartners/cache/1451807/1.2.html); [reply](https://stoneandtilepros.simplelists.com/sccpartners/cache/1451827/1.2.html)
- A mat retailer's outdoor rubber-backed mat page: "Moisture, sunlight, heat and cleaning products may cause discolouration to your flooring" — check with the flooring manufacturer before using a rubber-backed mat; "not using on light coloured and vinyl flooring" — [Dunelm Scrape N Sorb](https://dunelm.com/product/scrape-n-sorb-tile-outdoor-doormat-1000241671)

### Inferences
- Antioxidant staining is a reaction with an *organic finish* (PVC resin, wood lacquer); on a bare honed/polished calcareous stone there is no comparable polymer to react with, so the better-founded risk on stone is **plasticizer/oil migration into pores** (the Ren Collection claim) and **sulfur/residue transfer** (Baneclene, slate case), both producing a yellow-brown or dark shadow in the outline of the mat. This is extrapolation: no published, photographed case on marble/travertine was found.
- On the **dark anthracite screed**, if it carries a film-forming (acrylic) sealer the Euclid mechanism applies directly to *rubber* backings (sticky reaction, sealer lift); PVC is not "rubber", but PVC's plasticizers are known to soften acrylic/vinyl films, so a PVC backing on an acrylic-sealed screed is at least as suspect — treat as unverified but plausible.
- "Non-staining" is a real, labelled category (Armstrong's "nonstaining type of rubber"; "weichmacherfrei"/"phthalate-free" PVC). An unlabelled "special PVC backing to protect floors" is a marketing phrase, not that category.
- Heat is an accelerant in every mechanistic source (underfloor heating, temperature fluctuation, hot-weather sealer failure); a west-facing terrace in Cyprus summer is the worst case for a PVC backing on a sealed surface.

### Gaps
- No stone-industry technical bulletin (Natural Stone Institute/MIA, Lithofin, Fila, Akemi, Tenax, StoneTech, HG) was found that explains *why* rubber/PVC backings stain stone or distinguishes backing types; the NSI consumer page and Laticrete TDS 262 recommend entrance mats without any backing caveat (see Q3/Q4).
- No laboratory data were found comparing nitrile vs SBR vs PVC vs TPR backings for staining on any substrate; the nitrile-is-safer claim exists only as vendor marketing (nitrile "fade, stain and mildew resistant" — refers to the mat, not the floor).
- The BCA and Surface Care PROS documents could only be read as search excerpts (403 / access-denied on fetch), so their surrounding reasoning is unknown.

## 2. Moisture trapped under non-breathing mats on porous stone — efflorescence, water marks, mould/algae, shadowing, differential fading; recommended practice

### Takeaway
Trapped moisture is named by stone-restoration, mat-vendor and rug-trade sources as the *most common* cause of marks under mats; efflorescence is explicitly a water-transport phenomenon that worsens where evaporation is slowed; mould risk "is augmented beneath rugs". The universal recommendation is to lift mats regularly (weekly indoors; after wetting outdoors), never lay a mat on a damp floor, and use open/drainable or unbacked constructions outdoors. A documented "unweathered outline" (differential fading/cleaning line) under outdoor mats on stone was *not* found in authoritative sources — only forum cases of dye bleed and salt bloom.

### Cited Findings
- NSI consumer care: efflorescence "is caused by the deposition of mineral salts carried by water from below the surface of the stone"; "Do not use water to remove the powder; it will only temporarily disappear"; if it persists, find and remove the cause of the moisture — [Natural Stone Institute](https://www.naturalstoneinstitute.org/consumers/care/)
- Stone-restoration KB (excerpt only): take mats up at least once a week to avoid moisture build-up under them; trapped moisture under backings is what tends to cause the marble staining — [Surface Care PROS KB](https://sr-manual.com/kb/print-600.html)
- Mat vendor: "The most common culprit is moisture, which can become trapped under the mat if the floor is damp or the environment is humid"; "Never place mats on damp floors, as this invites mold, mildew, and warping"; let the floor dry completely before replacing the mat; clean under the mat frequently (wood/laminate named) — [Personalized Doormats](https://personalizeddoormats.com/blogs/news/what-causes-discoloration-under-floor-mats)
- Rug trade on marble: hazards listed as "Abrasive back scratches", "Moisture staining", "Chemical reactions"; "Risks of molds are augmented beneath rugs"; "Moisture control" is a marble priority — [Ren Collection](https://rencollection.com/blogs/news/rug-pads-hardwood-vs-marble-luxury-texas-floors)
- Rubber is air- and water-impermeable; mop water or condensate under a rubber-backed mat cannot escape (parquet context) — [antwort.net](https://www.antwort.net/a/antwort.net/warum-fuehren-manche-gummiruecken-von-fussmatten-zu-dauerhaften-verfaerbungen-auf-parkettboeden.html)
- Sealed concrete: a sealer "turned white" where "moisture trapped underneath the sealer has caused it to lose adhesion" — [Euclid Chemical](https://www.buildsite.com/pdf/euclid/REZ-SEAL-Technical-Notes-2150933.pdf)
- Outdoor rubber-backed mat, retailer care note: "regularly lifting mats to allow the floor underneath to be cleaned and aired"; use "inside or outside under shelter" — [Dunelm](https://dunelm.com/product/scrape-n-sorb-tile-outdoor-doormat-1000241671)
- PVC-backed coir outdoor mat, retailer care note (excerpt only): "Use in sheltered areas, avoid prolonged sun and water exposure, and prevent saturation to preserve its condition" — [B&Q Bedale PVC-backed coir](https://www.diy.com/departments/bedale-pvc-backed-printed-welcome-outdoor-coir-doormat-75-x-45cm/5056705303200_BQ.prd)
- 3M's matting brochure (excerpt only; PDF 403s): Nomad Terra 8100 is made "Without backing for drainage in outdoor areas", the 8150 variant "With vinyl backing to protect floors in indoor areas"; the "unbacked open construction of the mat allows water to drain and soil to pass through" — [3M Nomad brochure](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/1198543O/nomad-matting-brochure.pdf)
- Recessed wells: when a well is sealed at the bottom (common in retrofits) water collects between mat and well floor and causes breakdown and odour; absorbent inserts wick moisture through to the well floor; open-back designs let water and debris fall through (supplier material, excerpt) — [Canada Mats](https://canadamats.ca/pages/recessed-well-mats)
- Forum cases of outlines under outdoor mats: brown outline on a concrete porch after a straw mat soaked in rain (dye bleed) — [Talk of the Villages](https://www.talkofthevillages.com/forums/archive/index.php/t-101428.html); orange outline from a dyed straw mat on grey pavers — [Hometalk](https://www.hometalk.com/44113916/diy/tile); a mark under a doormat on a hearth diagnosed by posters as salt leaching from cheap concrete slabs when wet — [Mumsnet](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/property/4977831-whats-this-stain)
- Pavingexpert's page on circular/shade marks on patio flags attributes them to "differential curing" from stacking/shrink-wrap moisture at manufacture and says "there is no remedy other than time"; it does *not* describe mats or objects as a cause — [Pavingexpert](https://pavingexpert.com/faq_patio_circularmarks)

### Inferences
- Travertine and limestone are calcareous and (travertine especially) porous; a saturated absorbent mat (the 7 mm nylon mat is specified to hold 5.3 l/m²) lying on them after wind-blown winter rain is exactly the "slowed evaporation + dissolved salts" condition that drives efflorescence and dark damp shadows. This is a mechanistic inference; no travertine-specific case was found.
- A "cleaning line"/unweathered patch under an outdoor mat is plausible on an exterior stone that is otherwise weathering, bleaching in UV and being washed by rain, but the only documented outline cases found are dye bleed and salt bloom, not UV shading. Treat the fading-outline risk as unproven but cheap to avoid (reposition/rotate mats).
- On a *covered* west terrace in Cyprus, the mat under-side will cycle hot-dry in summer and cold-wet in winter; the open 15 mm unbacked mesh (type c) is the only one of the three candidates whose construction removes the moisture-trap mechanism by design.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source documents algae/mould or efflorescence specifically *under doormats on travertine or limestone*; the mechanism is assembled from general efflorescence guidance plus mat/rug-trade statements.
- No source quantifies how often an outdoor mat must be lifted; "regularly"/"weekly" are the only figures given.

## 3. Scratching and abrasion of polished marble by grit under a mat and by mats moving under foot; acceptable anti-slip measures on polished stone

### Takeaway
Grit is the main scratch agent on marble floors (it is harder than calcite), and the stone industry's own advice is to use mats *inside and outside* the entrance precisely to intercept it — with the proviso that the mat underside must be slip-resistant and the mat itself cleaned often so it does not become a grit-loaded sanding pad. Anti-slip layers judged stone-safe by the rug trade are felt, felt + natural rubber, and non-adhesive pads explicitly labelled non-staining; adhesive-backed pads, synthetic latex with clay fillers and rough latex/stiff canvas backings are the ones flagged for scratching or staining.

### Cited Findings
- NSI: "Mats or area rugs inside and outside an entrance will help to minimize the sand, dirt and grit that may scratch the stone floor. Be sure that the underside of the mat or rug is a slip resistant surface"; "Sand, dirt and grit are abrasive and can damage natural stone" — [Natural Stone Institute](https://www.naturalstoneinstitute.org/consumers/care/)
- Laticrete/StoneTech TDS 262 (rev. 14 Apr 2026): "abrasive particles of sand and dirt can damage natural stone and tile surfaces. Mats outside entrances will help to minimize damage"; "DO place a small rug or mat at entry ways to trap dirt and sand from normal foot traffic"; vacuum attachments and wheels "can scratch soft stone surfaces" — [Laticrete TDS 262](https://cdn-global.laticrete.com/-/media/project/laticrete-international/shared/files/support-and-downloads/technical-datasheets/tds262)
- Cleaning-trade marble guide: "DO use walk-off mats on polished marble entry floors"; "Grit is often harder than marble and is the main cause of scratches, especially in floors"; "Remember to wash off the mat frequently to remove the accumulated dirt"; no vacuum beater bar on marble — [Baneclene polished marble](https://www.baneclene.com/pages/polished-marble)
- Rug trade, marble: hazards include "Abrasive back scratches" and "Bruises due to heavy rugs"; natural rubber pads protect "against scratches on polished surfaces"; "stone-safe felt pads" give padding "without a lot of pressure and abrasion"; "Marble sealants can react with some low quality materials" — [Ren Collection](https://rencollection.com/blogs/news/rug-pads-hardwood-vs-marble-luxury-texas-floors)
- Rug-pad maker, marble: marble's slick surface makes rugs slide, natural-rubber backing recommended for grip; a solid thicker felt/rubber pad said to "fully protect marble floors from scratches"; avoid synthetic adhesives/glues — [RugPadUSA](https://www.rugpadusa.com/articles/protect-and-preserve-your-marble-floors-with-these-rug-pads)
- Rug-pad maker, tile incl. marble and stone (excerpt only): natural rubber does not break down or leave stains on tile flooring, including marble and stone — [RugPadUSA tile article](https://rugpadusa.com/articles/best-rug-pad-for-tile-floors)
- Vendor Q&A asked explicitly about travertine, saltillo and stained concrete: "No this pad is non-adhesive and safe for all hard surfaces and carpets" (material of the pad not specified on the page) — [Nourison FAQ](https://www.nourison.com/rug-pads-firmgrip-pad-pf21-ivory)
- Mat retailer: vinyl(PVC)-backed mats are "only available with a smooth backing" and "should only be used on hard surfaces such as wood, tile and marble"; rubber mats can "withstand extreme temperatures (both hot and cold)", vinyl does not have "the same resistance to extreme temperatures" — [American Floor Mats](http://www.americanfloormats.com/rubber-mats-versus-vinyl-mats/)

### Inferences
- The "sandpaper effect" is real in principle (grit under any mat that shifts), but the stone bodies treat it as the reason *for* mats, managed by cleaning the mat and the floor under it — not as a reason against mats. A smooth PVC back that stays put is less abrasive than a coir or vinyl-loop mat that creeps; the 9 mm coil mat (type b) has a PVC back for that reason, while the unbacked 15 mm mesh (type c) *will* creep on polished marble indoors and is only appropriate outdoors or in a well.
- Silicone dots/anti-slip sprays on polished stone were not covered by any source found; the rug trade's stone-safe answer is a felt + natural-rubber (non-staining, non-adhesive) underlay or a mat whose own backing is labelled non-staining. Anything adhesive is flagged.

### Gaps
- No source quantified abrasion from a moving vinyl-loop mat on polished marble; no source addressed silicone-dot anti-slip or sprays on stone.
- Nourison's "safe for all hard surfaces" is a vendor reply without material disclosure — low evidential weight.

## 4. Sealers — do penetrating sealers protect against mat staining; do topical sealers react with PVC/rubber; what stone-care brands advise about mats

### Takeaway
Stone-industry bodies say impregnating sealers only make stone "more stain resistant", never stain-proof, and buy time to wipe a spill; nothing found claims a penetrating sealer prevents migration staining or moisture shadowing from a mat. For film-forming sealers the evidence is clear on concrete: rubber additives can react with the sealer and turn it sticky, and the manufacturer's advice is not to seal where rubber will sit. None of the named stone-care brands (Fila, Lithofin, Akemi, Tenax, HG) publishes mat-specific guidance that could be found; StoneTech (Laticrete) and NSI simply recommend entrance mats.

### Cited Findings
- NSI: "the sealing products used in the stone industry are 'impregnators' which do not actually seal the stone"; "Sealing does not make the stone stain proof, rather it makes the stone more stain resistant"; many stones do not require sealing; consult the supplier/sealer maker on type and frequency — [Natural Stone Institute](https://www.naturalstoneinstitute.org/consumers/care/)
- Laticrete TDS 262: "Sealing will provide you with some time to wipe up a spill, but it cannot stop the chemical reaction that may leave an etch mark"; "DO use STONETECH Sealers to protect your stone, tile and cement based grouts" — no mat-backing caveat anywhere in the guide — [Laticrete TDS 262](https://cdn-global.laticrete.com/-/media/project/laticrete-international/shared/files/support-and-downloads/technical-datasheets/tds262)
- Baneclene: "DO replenish the sealer when spills no longer bead up on the marble" — [Baneclene](https://www.baneclene.com/pages/polished-marble)
- Euclid (film-forming concrete sealers): "Most concrete sealers will not prevent stains"; rubber additives "may chemically react with concrete sealers, resulting in the sealer becoming sticky"; "Do not seal area of concrete where rubber items will be in contact" — [Euclid Chemical](https://www.buildsite.com/pdf/euclid/REZ-SEAL-Technical-Notes-2150933.pdf)
- Rug trade: "Marble sealants can react with some low quality materials" (pad backings) — [Ren Collection](https://rencollection.com/blogs/news/rug-pads-hardwood-vs-marble-luxury-texas-floors)
- Lithofin (excerpt only): the Fleckstop PLUS data sheet's general rule is to test in an inconspicuous place in all cases of doubt; a stone-supplier care sheet describes impregnators such as Lithofin MN Stain-Stop as rendering the surface water- and oil-repellent but not protecting against acid — [Lithofin Fleckstop PLUS TDS](https://cdn.koempf24.de/product/8baffc/188-lithofin-fleckstop-plus-technisches-merkblatt.pdf); [Artedomus care sheet](https://www.artedomus.co.nz/_files/ugd/ccb493_43a6129762b14a72b73021ebe284c52b.pdf)
- Film-forming sealers on wet stone can lock moisture beneath them, whereas penetrating sealers keep pores open so moisture can evaporate (general stone-moisture article, excerpt) — [Litos Online](https://litosonline.com/en/article/invisible-dangers-beneath-natural-stone-how-rising-moisture-can-damage-natural-stone-floors)

### Inferences
- A good impregnator on travertine/limestone reduces how fast migrating plasticizer/oil or dye-laden water enters the pores, so it lowers the *severity* of a mat stain, but it does nothing against a damp shadow or efflorescence and the trade does not promise it prevents backing stains. Keep sealing, do not rely on it.
- Do not put a topical (acrylic/"wet-look") sealer on the terrace travertine or the anthracite screed under a backed mat; the Euclid mechanism (sticky reaction, white moisture blush) is documented for exactly that configuration on concrete, and PVC plasticizers are known softeners of acrylic films.

### Gaps
- No Fila, Lithofin, Akemi, Tenax or HG statement about mats or rug backings on stone was found (searches in English and German). The Surface Care PROS KB is the only stone-restoration source addressing mat backings, and only in excerpt.
- No source tests PVC (as opposed to rubber) against stone impregnators or acrylic stone sealers.

## 5. Manufacturer positions — backings claimed safe on stone/wood; caveats for outdoor use of PVC-backed nylon mats (heat, UV, moisture)

### Takeaway
Mat makers rarely say *why* a backing is "floor-safe"; the explicit, usable statements are: Armstrong's recognition of a "nonstaining" rubber category; flooring distributors' preference for non-staining *vinyl-backed* mats on vinyl; 3M's clear split between unbacked mats "for drainage in outdoor areas" and vinyl-backed mats "to protect floors in indoor areas"; the WaterHog line's standard SBR backing (nitrile only as an option) with a "resists cracking or curling compared with vinyl" claim; and retailers' consistent restriction of PVC-/rubber-backed textile mats to *sheltered* outdoor use with warnings that sun, heat and moisture may discolour the floor. Polyurethane and felt + natural rubber are the backings marketed as non-reactive.

### Cited Findings
- Armstrong: mats in contact with the floor should be of the "nonstaining" type of rubber; some mats are backed with materials that won't react — [Inquirer / Armstrong](https://www.inquirer.com/philly/business/real_estate/diy/20101119_Your_Place__Rubber_mats_may_turn_vinyl_flooring_yellow.html)
- Flooring distributor: "non-staining, vinyl-backed mats or woven rugs" recommended; rubber/latex-backed not — [Jacobsen](https://connect.jacobsen.co.nz/knowledge/can-i-use-rugs-or-mats-on-my-vinyl-floor)
- 3M (excerpt only): Nomad Terra 8100 "Without backing for drainage in outdoor areas"; 8150 "With vinyl backing to protect floors in indoor areas"; 8100 suitable "indoors, outdoors, or in recessed wells with high traffic volumes" — [3M Nomad brochure](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/1198543O/nomad-matting-brochure.pdf)
- WaterHog spec sheet: standard backing is 78-mil SBR rubber (cleated or smooth), "available with optional Anchor Safe® nitrile rubber backing on classic border mat only"; the rubber backing is said to lie flat and resist cracking or curling compared with vinyl mats; UV claims concern the face fibre, not the backing — [WaterHog Classic spec sheet](https://www.americanfloormats.com/content/product/file/document/Waterhog%20Classic%20Spec%20Sheet.pdf)
- Mat retailer: rubber (nitrile) mats withstand extreme hot and cold, vinyl does not have the same temperature resistance; vinyl-backed mats are smooth-backed and for hard floors "such as wood, tile and marble"; retailer prefers rubber-backed overall — [American Floor Mats](http://www.americanfloormats.com/rubber-mats-versus-vinyl-mats/)
- Dunelm (rubber-backed nylon outdoor mat): "inside or outside under shelter"; lift regularly to clean and air the floor; not on light-coloured or vinyl floors; "Moisture, sunlight, heat and cleaning products may cause discolouration to your flooring" — [Dunelm](https://dunelm.com/product/scrape-n-sorb-tile-outdoor-doormat-1000241671)
- B&Q (PVC-backed coir, excerpt only): "Use in sheltered areas, avoid prolonged sun and water exposure, and prevent saturation" — [B&Q](https://www.diy.com/departments/bedale-pvc-backed-printed-welcome-outdoor-coir-doormat-75-x-45cm/5056705303200_BQ.prd)
- Polyurethane-mat vendor: "Any rubber or PVC-backed mat left in place long enough will damage the floor beneath it"; polyurethane "does not leach chemicals into floor finishes" (uncertified vendor claim) — [WellnessMats](https://www.wellnessmats.com/blogs/the-wellness-journal/rubber-backed-mats-damage-floors)
- Rug-pad makers: felt and natural rubber (no glue, no PVC) marketed as safe for marble/stone; synthetic latex with clay fillers flagged — [RugPadUSA](https://www.rugpadusa.com/articles/protect-and-preserve-your-marble-floors-with-these-rug-pads)
- German market: product descriptions advertise a "phthalatfreie Rückenbeschichtung" and underfloor-heating suitability as the relevant floor-safety labels; "für Parkett geeignet"/"weichmacherfrei" are the labels to look for (excerpt + explainer) — [antwort.net](https://www.antwort.net/a/antwort.net/warum-fuehren-manche-gummiruecken-von-fussmatten-zu-dauerhaften-verfaerbungen-auf-parkettboeden.html)

### Inferences
- "Special PVC backing to protect floors" (mat a) most likely means *smooth, non-marking, non-slip* in the American-Floor-Mats sense; it is not a non-staining/phthalate-free declaration unless the datasheet says so. Ask the maker in writing: plasticizer type (phthalate-free?), "non-staining" declaration, max surface temperature, and whether outdoor (even covered) use is warranted.
- Retailers' "sheltered only / avoid prolonged sun" caveats for PVC-backed textile mats are about the mat's own durability (backing embrittlement, curl) as much as the floor; either way they argue against a PVC-backed absorbent mat on a west-facing Cyprus terrace.

### Gaps
- No manufacturer statement was found that explicitly warrants a PVC- or rubber-backed mat as safe on *natural stone*; stone appears only in retailer phrasing ("hard surfaces such as wood, tile and marble").
- No published surface-temperature limit for PVC mat backings was found.

## 6. Alternative threshold solutions for stone houses — recessed wells with drainage, brass/stainless frames, stone-set coir, drip trays

### Takeaway
The architectural answer is a recessed matwell with a flush metal (brass/aluminium/stainless) frame set level with the stone and a drainable insert (open-back/unbacked loop or grid, or coir in a frame sized to its thickness); suppliers stress that a well sealed at the bottom becomes a water trap, so outdoors the well needs a fall or a drain. Brass matwell frames are sold for 17 mm coir or 12 mm ribbed inserts; drip trays and "stone-set coir" were not found as documented products.

### Cited Findings
- Recessed wells: sealed-bottom wells collect water between mat and floor → breakdown and odour; open-back inserts let moisture and dirt pass through; a well's height can be built up with screed or marine ply; standard inserts seat in wells ~10–11 mm (3/8–7/16 in) deep (supplier material, excerpts) — [Canada Mats](https://canadamats.ca/pages/recessed-well-mats); [Mats Inc. recessed inserts](https://mats-inc.com/product/recessed-entry-mats-floor-grates/recessed-mat-inserts.html)
- For multi-well grate systems one manufacturer recommends one drainage system per well unit, with drainage connections on request (excerpt) — [emco Bautechnik via ArchiExpo](https://www.archiexpo.com/prod/emco-bautechnik-gmbh/product-54726-2184779.html)
- Frame profile is specified to match the floor course it meets — tile edge, stone surround or carpet transition (excerpt) — [Classic Architectural Group](https://classic-arch.com/articles/entrance-mats-importance-accurate-mat-wells)
- Brass matwell frames: Premier's brass frames come in two options, "one for a 17mm thick coir mat and the other for a 12mm thick ribbed mat"; mitred corners fixed with an angle connector; for fixed (not floating) floors — [Stair-Rods Direct matwell frames](https://stair-rodsdirect.co.uk/door-bars/matt-well-frame/brass-colours)
- Installation: "place angle in the recess and set angles level with adjoining floor surfaces" (Birrus); chalk the well outline onto the mat backing, cut slightly oversize then trim — [First Mats fitting guide](https://www.firstmats.co.uk/blogs/buying-guides/how-to-fit-a-recessed-door-mat)
- 3M's unbacked 8100 loop matting is specified for "recessed wells" as well as outdoors; its open construction drains and lets soil fall through (excerpt) — [3M Nomad brochure](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/1198543O/nomad-matting-brochure.pdf)
- Coir listings themselves advise a sheltered entrance such as a covered porch and avoiding prolonged direct sun, standing water or excessive moisture (retail excerpt) — [Cotswold Co. coir listing](https://www.cotswoldco.com/homeware/soft-furnishings/doormats/wrought-iron--coir)

### Inferences
- For an existing finished marble/travertine floor a retrofit well means cutting stone; the realistic retrofit equivalents are (i) a loose open-grid/unbacked mat outdoors (drains, no backing chemistry, lifted easily), and (ii) indoors a light, cloth-/felt-backed or declared non-staining mat that is lifted weekly. A loose coir mat on a tray is undocumented and, given coir's dye-bleed and water-holding behaviour in the forum cases, not an improvement outdoors.

### Gaps
- No source on "stone-set coir" or drip-tray thresholds; no stone-specific guidance on well drainage detailing beyond supplier generalities.

## 7. What applies to the Paphos house (synthesis; incl. a practical care routine)

### Takeaway
Indoors on honed/polished marble the documented risk from mat (a)'s PVC backing is low-to-moderate and mostly moisture-driven; it is acceptable with a weekly lift and a written non-staining/phthalate-free confirmation from the maker. Outdoors the picture reverses: an absorbent PVC-backed mat holding up to 5.3 l/m² on porous travertine/limestone under a west-facing Cyprus sun — hot-dry in summer, rain-soaked for days in winter — combines every accelerant named in the sources (trapped water, heat, UV, a plasticized backing on a calcareous, absorbent stone). The open 15 mm unbacked mesh (c) is the construction the trade itself specifies "for drainage in outdoor areas"; the 9 mm PVC-backed coil mat (b) is a middle option only if it is lifted after rain. On the dark sealed screed at the kitchen side door avoid any *rubber* backing outright (documented sticky-sealer reaction) and treat PVC as suspect unless the sealer is penetrating, not film-forming.

### Cited Findings
- (Evidence base for each line above is cited in sections 1–6; the key anchors are the Euclid troubleshooting guide for sealed concrete — [Euclid](https://www.buildsite.com/pdf/euclid/REZ-SEAL-Technical-Notes-2150933.pdf); NSI/Laticrete for grit management and sealer limits — [NSI](https://www.naturalstoneinstitute.org/consumers/care/), [Laticrete TDS 262](https://cdn-global.laticrete.com/-/media/project/laticrete-international/shared/files/support-and-downloads/technical-datasheets/tds262); the Surface Care PROS weekly-lift / cloth-back advice for marble (excerpt) — [SCP KB](https://sr-manual.com/kb/article/what-do-i-tell-my-customers-when-they-ask-what-type-of-floor-mats-do-i-recommend-for-use-on-a-marble-floor-i-was-told-to-stay-away-from-rubber-backed-mats-600.html); the sheltered-only / lift-regularly / sun-heat-moisture caveats on backed outdoor mats — [Dunelm](https://dunelm.com/product/scrape-n-sorb-tile-outdoor-doormat-1000241671), [B&Q](https://www.diy.com/departments/bedale-pvc-backed-printed-welcome-outdoor-coir-doormat-75-x-45cm/5056705303200_BQ.prd); 3M's unbacked-for-outdoor-drainage split — [3M](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/1198543O/nomad-matting-brochure.pdf); and the rug trade on calcareous stone absorbing migrating oils — [Ren Collection](https://rencollection.com/blogs/news/best-rug-pads-in-dallas-tx-protect-your-floors-and-rugs).)

### Inferences
- **Indoors (marble, 100×110 front door; 70×60 balcony door):** mat (a) is workable. Conditions: obtain the maker's written statement that the PVC backing is phthalate-free/non-staining and intended for natural stone; lay it only on a dry floor; lift it weekly, dust-mop under it, let both dry; shake/vacuum the mat often (it is the grit trap); run a 6–8-week test with the mat in place, then inspect the stone in raking light for a shadow or dulling in the mat's outline before committing. If any outline appears, switch to a cloth-/felt-backed mat (the restoration trade's recommendation for marble) and keep lifting weekly. An unbacked loop mat (c) does not belong indoors on polished marble (it creeps and sheds grit).
- **Covered terrace, west-facing (200×100 in front of the sliding doors) and the three covered balconies (100×60):** prefer (c), the open unbacked vinyl mesh — it drains, does not seal moisture against the travertine/limestone, has no backing to migrate or stick in heat, and is light enough to lift and hose. It will move on smooth stone, so size it generously and accept that it is a walk-off scraper, not an absorbent. If an absorbent is wanted at the sliding doors, keep it *inside* on the marble (mat a) rather than outside on the stone. Mat (b) is acceptable on the balconies only with the discipline below. Do not lay mat (a) outdoors: it is the configuration (absorbent + impermeable PVC back + porous calcareous stone + heat/UV) that every source argues against.
- **Front door outside (100×80 on travertine/limestone, covered):** same logic — (c) first, (b) second; under no circumstances a rubber-backed coir.
- **Kitchen side door (70×60 on dark anthracite sealed screed/stone):** find out what sealer is on it. If it is a film-forming (acrylic/epoxy/"wet-look") sealer, no rubber-backed mat (documented sticky reaction, sealer lift under solvent cleaning) and PVC only on trial; if it is a penetrating sealer, (b) or (c) with the lift routine. The dark colour hides a shadow, which cuts both ways: a stain will show late and be hard to remove with solvents without touching the sealer.
- **Sealing:** keep the exterior travertine/limestone impregnated (re-apply when water stops beading) — it slows absorption of whatever a mat releases — but do not read it as protection; never apply a topical sealer where a backed mat will sit.
- **Care routine (practical):** Indoors — lift weekly, dust-mop/damp-mop the stone with a neutral stone cleaner, let dry, re-lay; vacuum/shake mats twice a week in the dusty season. Outdoors — after every rain or hosing, stand the mat on edge until the stone under it is dry (winter: expect days); in summer, lift and move the terrace mat by a tile-width every few weeks so no single outline bakes in; hose the unbacked mesh through and let the stone dry before re-laying; monthly, inspect under every mat in low sun for a darker/lighter outline, white salt bloom, green film or tackiness, and act on the first sign (dry, neutral-clean, reposition; for salt bloom brush dry, do not wash); replace any mat whose backing becomes tacky, crumbly or curled — that is the point at which migration and sticking are reported. Keep an off-cut tile of the terrace stone under one corner of the terrace mat for the first summer as a sacrificial test coupon.

### Gaps
- Everything stone-specific above is inference from vinyl/wood/concrete mechanisms plus trade cautions; no published case of a PVC-backed nylon mat staining marble, travertine or limestone was found, and none refuting it either.
- The actual composition (plasticizer type, antioxidant package) of the three candidate mats' backings is unknown; a maker's datasheet or written reply is the only way to close that.
