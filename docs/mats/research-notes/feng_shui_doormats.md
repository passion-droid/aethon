# Feng Shui and doormats — classical schools vs modern Western / BTB practice (sourced note)

Scope: what Feng Shui literature (classical compass/form schools, Lillian Too's World of Feng Shui, modern BTB / Western practitioners, Cliff Tan of Dear Modern, Chinese- and Japanese-language popular sources in English summary) says about doormats — colour by door direction and the five elements, shape, size, material, words/names/symbols, inside vs outside, condition — where the schools differ, and which points bear on a west-coast Paphos residence whose front door is on the (presumed) east street side.

Research caveat up front: there is **no classical text on doormats**. Every colour-by-direction table below is a modern extrapolation of the five-element sector map onto an object that classical manuals never mention; the classical sources I could reach (World of Feng Shui, Master Sean Chan, fengshuied.com) talk about the *door*, the *bright hall* and the *approach*, and say nothing about mats at all. The most specific "rules" come from Western lifestyle/BTB writers and from retailers. Treat the tables as practitioner conventions, not doctrine. Sources disagree on details (see the contradictions flagged inline), and one commercial source states plainly that "there is no scientific evidence to support this practice" — [Personalized Doormats](https://personalizeddoormats.com/blogs/news/no-bad-luck-here-explaining-common-doormat-superstitions).

## 1. Colour by compass direction of the door and the five elements (productive vs controlling cycles; green at a west door, earth tones / grey at an east door)

### Takeaway
Where sources give a table at all, they agree on the core: **east/south-east = Wood → greens (plus blues/black as Water, which feeds Wood); west/north-west = Metal → white, grey, metallics, with earth tones (beige, sand, ochre) as the element that produces Metal; south = Fire → reds; north = Water → black/blue**. The commonly repeated "avoid" rule is the controlling cycle: don't put the colour of the element that *destroys* the door's element on the door (fire reds at a west/Metal door; metal whites/greys at an east/Wood door). A green mat at a west door and a beige/khaki mat at an east door are each "off-palette but not the forbidden colour"; a light-grey mat at an east door is the one combination the controlling-cycle logic actually argues against — though one Chinese popular source lists grey *for* east, so even that is contested. Classical practitioners key colour to the *sector* or to the occupant's *Kua*, use it only as an accent, and some do not use colour as a cure at all.

### Cited Findings

**Five-element tables as applied to doormats (modern/Western sources)**
- Benna Crawford (LoveToKnow, five-element approach, with the black-mat rule attributed to the Black Hat school): **West/North-west (Metal):** "Metal's colors are white, gray, metallics and, to a lesser extent, earth colors." **East/South-east (Wood):** greens and browns, "plus blues, blacks, yellows, and earth shades for support." **South (Fire):** reds, purples, deep oranges, vivid yellows, deep pinks; greens and browns also work "because wood feeds fire." **North (Water):** blues and blacks, some whites and greys. **South-west/North-east (Earth):** sandy, clay and soil shades plus reds, pinks, yellows, purples. General rule: match or harmonise the mat with the door colour; where exact colours don't fit, "use patterned mats or neutral mats with colored borders." — [LoveToKnow, Feng Shui Rules for Door Mats](https://feng-shui.maint.lovetoknow.com/feng-shui-home/feng-shui-door-mats)
- A retailer blog gives the same element-colour mapping for mats (Wood: greens/blues; Fire: reds; Earth: brown/orange/yellow; Metal: metallic hues, silver; Water: dark blue/black) but names no practitioner and closes with "go with your gut." — [New England Trading Co.](https://thenewenglandtradingcompany.com/blogs/default-blog/front-door-feng-shui-create-a-positive-flow-with-the-right-outdoor-mat)
- Short-form directional list repeated across lifestyle sites: "Red for South, black for North, green for East, and white for West." — [Uchify, Eldee Goh](https://uchify.com/feng-shui-front-door-facing/); and "red or purple for south-facing doors; blue or black for north-facing; green for east-facing; white or gold for west-facing" together with "more importantly, the doormat should be kept clean" — reported in search results citing Japanese/Chinese sources (snippet only; see Gaps).
- A Japanese tatami-maker's feng shui guide gives a **different** eight-direction mat table: North white/pink/orange/blue; North-east grey/purple/red; **East green/blue**; South-east pink/orange/green; South red/purple; South-west red/yellow/purple; **West beige/yellow**; North-west brown/yellow/blue — i.e. it puts the *producing* element's colours (Earth for West, Water-ish blue for East) in the sector rather than the sector's own element colours. — [Ikehiko Japan](https://www.ikehikojapan.com/blogs/product/g_20220201-6)

**Chinese-language popular sources (summarised in English)**
- A Chinese household-feng-shui article: "如果大门朝东开，那么地垫的颜色可以选择绿色或蓝色" (if the main door faces east, the mat may be green or blue — these colours "represent vigour and vitality"); and the governing rule: "地垫的颜色不能与大门方位相冲突，要注意地垫的颜色和大门方位的五行属性是否相符" (the mat's colour must not conflict with the door's direction; check it matches the Five-Element attribute of the door's direction). It names no colours to avoid. — [k366.com 华易网](https://m.k366.com/wenzhang/jiaju/195357.htm)
- A Toutiao article (page itself returned 404; content from the search index) maps: East/South-east → blue, green, **grey**; South → green, blue; North-east/South-west → yellow, red; North → white, grey; North-west → yellow, white; and adds a Flying-Star layer that changes yearly (e.g. avoid red/yellow mats under the 2 and 5 stars, use a grey mat with coins; red under the 3 star). — [Toutiao](https://www.toutiao.com/zixun/7544553064238368794/) (unverified beyond snippet). **Contradiction:** this is the only source putting grey at an east door; the controlling-cycle logic (Metal cuts Wood) argues the opposite.

**Productive and controlling cycles applied to doors**
- Homedit on a west-facing house: "The west section coincides with the metal element"; "the earth element supports and strengthens the metal element"; recommended at the entrance: "light shades like white or gray", metallic shades (silver, gold, copper), and "Warmer tones like beige and dusty yellow and even stained doors represent the earth element." Avoid "vibrant reds, oranges, and purples" because they "can overstimulate metal energy and create confusion" — fire is destructive to metal in the elemental cycle. — [Homedit](https://www.homedit.com/feng-shui/west-facing-house)
- Real Simple (via Yahoo): "A west-facing home is enhanced by metal or earth element colors"; for East, "Work with water or wood element colors … Wood is related to greens, mid-tone blues, and teals"; its only explicit avoid-list is for north-facing doors ("skip greens, browns, yellows, red, purple, orange, and deep pink"). — [Real Simple / Yahoo](https://shopping.yahoo.com/home-garden/home-decor/articles/pick-front-door-color-feng-222050292.html)
- Cliff Tan (book summary): "Main door colour depends upon direction. For North, use Water black to blend in. For South, use Fire bright colours." His element table: Fire red/orange south; Earth yellow/brown centre; **Metal white, west**; Water black/deep blue north; **Wood green/light blue, east**. He also notes colour reading is interpretive — "A grey-blue reads as water, while a very saturated blue reads as wood" — and that lower saturation feels calmer. — [Feng Shui Modern, personal summary](https://syedtalha.bearblog.dev/feng-shui-modern-by-cliff-tan-a-personalised-summary/)

**Classical / compass-school positions (colour as accent, keyed to sector or Kua, or not used)**
- Master Sean Chan (classical, Singapore), on a front door in the West sector: "The West sector's palette is white and silver, drawn from its metal element" — but "in a draining placement, you want to soften the sector's element rather than amplify it. … Lead with the room's neutral tones and use the sector's colours only as small accents." (He also classes a door in the West *sector* of the plan as "a placement to avoid where possible" on Fire-controls-Metal grounds — sector analysis, not compass-facing of the house.) — [Master Sean Chan](https://www.masterseanchan.com/fengshui-front-door-facing-west/)
- World of Feng Shui (Lillian Too's organisation) keys door colour and shape to the **occupant's Kua number**, not the door's compass facing: Kua 6 → West, round door, white; Kua 7 → North-west, round, white; Kua 9 → East, rectangular, green/brown; Kua 1 → South-east, rectangular, green/brown; Kua 2/5/8 → square, earth/ochre; Kua 3 → South, triangular, red/maroon; Kua 4 → North, wavy, black/blue. To *energise* a West/North-west door it recommends golden rocks near the door; for East/South-east a water feature on the left looking out. No mats are mentioned. — [WOFS, Always Look After the Feng Shui of Your Front Door](https://www.wofs.com/?p=5307)

### Inferences
- Reading the tables against the house: an **east (Wood) front door** is "on-palette" with greens (own element) and blues/blacks (Water feeds Wood); **dark-green lettering** is therefore the most orthodox element on the planned mats. A **khaki/beige (Earth) base** at an east door is not in Ikehiko's east list but *is* in LoveToKnow's ("earth shades for support"); by the cycle Earth is the element Wood *controls*, so it is "off-palette, not forbidden." A **light-grey base** at an east door is the one combination the controlling cycle (Metal cuts Wood) counts against — unless the grey is read as stone/Earth rather than Metal, which the sources leave open (colour classification is interpretive per Cliff Tan).
- A **west (Metal) terrace/balcony door** is on-palette with light stone, white, grey, and beige/sand (Earth produces Metal — Ikehiko even lists West = beige/yellow); **green** there is the element Metal controls — again off-palette, not the forbidden fire red. Sean Chan's and Cliff Tan's logic (neutral ground, sector colour only as a small accent, low saturation) both support a light stone mat with a *small* dark-green mark rather than a green field.
- The "forbidden" colour per the controlling cycle is therefore red/orange/purple at west doors and white/grey/metallic at east doors — none of the planned bases is fire-coloured, so the only point to raise with the consultant is the light-grey base if it goes on the east door.

### Gaps
- No source explicitly discusses "a green mat at a west door" or "an earth-tone mat at an east door"; the readings above are derived from the tables and the cycle.
- The widely quoted line that "classical feng shui doesn't typically recognize that a color can activate an element" appeared only in a search summary; I could not locate the page or author, so it is reported here as an unattributed claim (consistent with Sean Chan's "accents only" and WOFS's Kua-based approach, but not confirmed as a quotation).
- The Spruce (Rodika Tchi / Anjie Cho) blocks the crawler, so its doormat-colour guidance could not be read.

## 2. Shape, size, material, condition; one mat vs two; back doors, balcony doors and sliding glass doors to the garden

### Takeaway
Size is the most consistent rule — the mat should be as wide as, or slightly wider than, the door, never so big it crowds the entrance and never so thick it impedes the door swing. Shape is element-keyed only in the five-element school (west/Metal = square or round, east/Wood = rectangular), while BTB writers simply say "rectangular". Natural fibres are favoured (yin, slow and gather chi); plastics are said to repel chi; opinions on rubber split. Condition is unanimous: a worn or dirty mat is "depleted chi" and must be replaced. Classical sources treat back and balcony doors as secondary; no source prescribes mats for them, and a sliding glass wall onto one's own garden is classed as a "milder concern" or not a true back door at all.

### Cited Findings

**Size**
- "As a feng shui rule of thumb, welcome mats should be slightly wider than your door" — [Dorena Kohrs, Space Doula / House Talk](https://spacedoula.substack.com/p/how-important-is-your-front-door)
- Anjie Cho's *108 Ways* (as summarised): a new doormat "in red or black if possible and, ideally, it should be the same width as the door." — [Yorkshire Post](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/news/how-to-create-a-mindful-and-peaceful-home-556306) (page 403'd on fetch; wording from the search index)
- Michele Duffy (BTB M.F.S., "Ancient Bon Buddhist Feng Shui lineage"): "A fresh welcome doormat should match the size of your front door." — [Lamorinda Weekly](https://lamorindaweekly.com/archive/issue1306/Feng-Shui-Front-entrance-refresh.html)
- Black Hat rule: "A black mat roughly as wide as the door is said to attract money and energize your career." Interior mats must be "large enough so guests step fully in on the mat," slip-resistant, and "low enough for the door to swing freely." — [LoveToKnow](https://feng-shui.maint.lovetoknow.com/feng-shui-home/feng-shui-door-mats)
- "If your outdoor mat is too big, your front door will look crowded"; a 24" × 36" mat "usually settles in very well" on a 36" door; test proportions by chalking both sizes on the step. — [New England Trading Co.](https://thenewenglandtradingcompany.com/blogs/default-blog/front-door-feng-shui-create-a-positive-flow-with-the-right-outdoor-mat)
- Anjie Cho (BTB): "Sometimes people even end up choosing a thick doormat that makes it hard to open the door." — [Anjie Cho / Livingetc](https://www.anjiecho.com/holistic-spaces-blog/2024/1/how-to-declutter-for-better-feng-shui-3-areas-experts-focus-on-for-free-flowing-qi)

**Shape**
- Five-element shapes for mats: South/Fire angular (triangles, stars); North/Water round or wavy; **West/North-west/Metal "square or round, with strong symmetry"**; **East/South-east/Wood rectangular**; Earth square. — [LoveToKnow](https://feng-shui.maint.lovetoknow.com/feng-shui-home/feng-shui-door-mats)
- Ikehiko: rectangle = Earth/stability; oval = amicable relationships, money; circle = gathering energy; avoid "strong fire-element patterns such as flat triangles, diamonds, and stars." — [Ikehiko Japan](https://www.ikehikojapan.com/blogs/product/g_20220201-6)
- Anjie Cho (BTB): "I often recommend putting down a nice black rectangular welcome mat to attract good energy." — [mindbodygreen](https://www.mindbodygreen.com/articles/feng-shui-for-every-room-in-your-home)
- WOFS keys door *shape* to Kua (West/North-west round; East/South-east rectangular; Earth sectors square). — [WOFS](https://www.wofs.com/?p=5307)

**Material**
- LoveToKnow's yin/yang reading: natural fibres (rush, cork, wicker, coir, bamboo) are **yin — they slow chi** and suit an entrance where energy already flows well or a too-straight path; polished hardwood and metal grids are **yang — they speed chi** past "poison arrows"; "Plastic tends to repel chi"; rubber "symbolizes abundance and wealth." — [LoveToKnow](https://feng-shui.maint.lovetoknow.com/feng-shui-home/feng-shui-door-mats)
- Ikehiko: prefer "natural materials such as cotton, linen, and wool"; chemical fibres belong to Fire and may "burn away the good chi" — 100% natural where possible. — [Ikehiko Japan](https://www.ikehikojapan.com/blogs/product/g_20220201-6)
- Renters' tip: "If possible, choose a mat made of natural material." — [BellaOnline](https://www.bellaonline.com/articles/art34513.asp) (snippet)
- Contrary retail view: coir and rubber "shed, crack, and wear down"; a durable mat "symbolises longevity, which in feng shui is linked to good fortune staying longer." — [New England Trading Co.](https://thenewenglandtradingcompany.com/blogs/default-blog/front-door-feng-shui-create-a-positive-flow-with-the-right-outdoor-mat)

**Condition**
- "A tatty old doormat shows your lack of care and attention and is bad news"; "Every foot-wipe deposits negative Chi on your mat, so the longer you hang on to your old doormat, the longer you're hosting all those negative energies." — [Rex London](https://www.rexlondon.com/blog/feng-shui-door-mat)
- Anjie Cho: "Another thing that can hamper the flow of qi is a doormat that is accumulating its own clutter and debris." — [Anjie Cho](https://www.anjiecho.com/holistic-spaces-blog/2024/1/how-to-declutter-for-better-feng-shui-3-areas-experts-focus-on-for-free-flowing-qi); the front door needs cleaning "interior side, exterior side, and the edge." — [Anjie Cho, front door](https://www.anjiecho.com/holistic-spaces-blog/2020/1/feng-shui-front-door)
- Chinese source: "要避免长期不清洗地垫 … 脏污的地垫只会带入更多不良气场" (don't leave the mat unwashed; a dirty mat only brings in more bad energy). — [k366.com](https://m.k366.com/wenzhang/jiaju/195357.htm)
- Replace faded or worn seasonal mats; mismatched seasonal mats "suggest carelessness." — [LoveToKnow](https://feng-shui.maint.lovetoknow.com/feng-shui-home/feng-shui-door-mats)

**One mat vs two (outside + inside)**
- "Exterior mats are fine, but an inside complementary area rug is often preferred over a second mat"; an interior mat "must balance the home's decor and harmonize with the entry"; walk-off mats inside the door are justified for heavy traffic, dusty/leafy approaches and delicate floors. — [LoveToKnow](https://feng-shui.maint.lovetoknow.com/feng-shui-home/feng-shui-door-mats)
- An upside-down (face-down) mat is "taboo in certain societies" (folk belief, unnamed). — [Personalized Doormats](https://personalizeddoormats.com/blogs/news/no-bad-luck-here-explaining-common-doormat-superstitions)

**Back doors, balcony doors, sliding glass walls**
- fengshuied.com (classical): "the back door should never upstage the front door"; "the front door gobbles up chi for the house, and the back door releases it"; a rear door "opening onto a pool, playground, or backyard within the property may not count" as a back door at all; doors to indoor areas should open inward, and opening outward is acceptable only "when it opens to other outdoor area of the house such as garden or patios"; keep the doorway "clear of clutter or any other form of obstruction." No mats mentioned. — [fengshuied.com, back door](https://www.fengshuied.com/?p=3410)
- Master Sean Chan: a back sliding door or large window aligned with the entry is a "Milder concern" because "a non-operating window doesn't actively channel qi the way a frequently used door does"; the underlying worry is the house becoming "a tube rather than a vessel." — [Master Sean Chan, front/back door myth](https://www.masterseanchan.com/feng-shui-front-back-door-myth/)
- Cliff Tan (book summary): "French doors are not considered as doors." — [Feng Shui Modern summary](https://syedtalha.bearblog.dev/feng-shui-modern-by-cliff-tan-a-personalised-summary/)
- Anjie Cho (BTB): the *formal* front door is the mouth of qi; secondary doors used daily are acceptable, "but the formal door is the one that counts"; open and use it at least every nine days. — [Anjie Cho](https://www.anjiecho.com/holistic-spaces-blog/2020/1/feng-shui-front-door)

### Inferences
- For the house: a mat the full width of the leaf (or slightly wider), low-profile, lying flat and not fouling the swing, satisfies every school; a semicircle would only be "on-element" at a north/Water door and is nowhere recommended for east or west.
- The west terrace and balcony mats fall outside what any school regulates: the terrace glass wall opens onto the house's own garden and pool, which fengshuied would not even count as a back door, and Sean Chan/Cliff Tan class sliding/French glazing as non-doors. Their mats are a practical, not a feng-shui, decision — except that the "no worn mats, no clutter at thresholds" rule still applies.
- Two mats at the front door (travertine outside, marble inside) are acceptable in all sources; LoveToKnow's preference for an inside *rug* over a second *mat* is about decor harmony, which a marble-toned mat already meets.

### Gaps
- No source addresses balcony-door mats specifically.
- Chinese practical advice "门外挡大脏，门内抓细灰" (outside catches coarse dirt, inside fine dust) surfaced only as a search snippet; the page I fetched did not contain it, so it is unverified.

## 3. Words, names and symbols on mats ("Welcome", monograms, family or house names, the dot/sun symbol; dark lettering on light ground)

### Takeaway
The dominant modern view — Cliff Tan's included — is that a mat should not carry anything you *revere* (a name, monogram, admired logo or symbol), because visitors wipe their feet on it; a minority (retail-leaning) view says a personalised mat signals permanence and self-expression. "Welcome" mats are accepted by BTB writers and are only questioned in folk superstition. No feng shui source discusses lettering contrast (dark on light vs light on dark); the only pattern guidance favours quiet nature motifs and symmetrical Metal shapes (circle, square) at west doors and warns off fire shapes (triangles, stars) and realistic animals/people.

### Cited Findings
- **Cliff Tan (Dear Modern), IKEA feature "Feng shui the modern way":** "Avoid a doormat with a logo of something you admire, because why would you want to trample on that?" — [IKEA Life at Home](https://lifeathome.ikea.com/?p=12253) (the page now redirects to IKEA's stories hub; quotation from the search index of the original article). His book, as summarised: "Entry mat must not display regarded symbols. Trust your instincts for designs and impressions intended." — [Feng Shui Modern summary](https://syedtalha.bearblog.dev/feng-shui-modern-by-cliff-tan-a-personalised-summary/)
- LoveToKnow (five-element / Black Hat): "Forget cutesy sayings and monogrammed mats" — don't put your name or initials on the mat "since guests will wipe their shoes on it"; examples to avoid: "No One's Home!", "Beware The Cat!". — [LoveToKnow](https://feng-shui.maint.lovetoknow.com/feng-shui-home/feng-shui-door-mats)
- Space Doula: mats "shouldn't be monogrammed. We don't want you stepping all over yourself or your reputation." — [Space Doula](https://spacedoula.substack.com/p/how-important-is-your-front-door)
- **Opposing view:** "A personalised, pretty doormat gives a sense of permanence to your home"; choose "a doormat that really expresses who you are and what you love." — [Rex London](https://www.rexlondon.com/blog/feng-shui-door-mat) (retailer blog)
- "Welcome" mats: BTB writers recommend them without hesitation — "Put out a welcome mat, preferably one with some red in it because red is a powerful chi enhancer" ([BC Living](https://bcliving.ca/health-fitness/general/feng-shui-practices-home-entryway/)); Anjie Cho recommends "a nice black rectangular welcome mat" ([mindbodygreen](https://www.mindbodygreen.com/articles/feng-shui-for-every-room-in-your-home)). Only folk superstition objects: in "certain parts of the world" some "see the word 'welcome' as an open door that you should avoid" — presented as "old tales." — [Personalized Doormats](https://personalizeddoormats.com/blogs/news/no-bad-luck-here-explaining-common-doormat-superstitions)
- Motifs: prefer nature motifs (flowers, plants, trees, fruit); "avoid human or animal motifs" (if used, stylised not realistic); avoid Fire shapes (triangles, diamonds, stars). — [Ikehiko Japan](https://www.ikehikojapan.com/blogs/product/g_20220201-6)
- Symmetrical square/round forms belong to Metal (west); circle = "gathering energy." — [LoveToKnow](https://feng-shui.maint.lovetoknow.com/feng-shui-home/feng-shui-door-mats); [Ikehiko Japan](https://www.ikehikojapan.com/blogs/product/g_20220201-6)
- Sun at the door is read positively: "When your front door captures the sun, it's thought to help you capture fame, fortune and longevity." — [Houzz AU](https://www.houzz.com.au/ideabooks/26037470/thumbs/feng-shui-13-ways-to-bring-positive-energy-to-your-front-entrance)
- Door signage: the house number "should be clean, hung straight, and easy to see" because "auspicious opportunities need clear signage to find you" (Anjie Cho) — identity belongs on the door/wall, not underfoot. — [mindbodygreen](https://www.mindbodygreen.com/articles/feng-shui-for-every-room-in-your-home)
- Cliff Tan's design-side comments relevant to a light ground with dark mark: "White is not a base colour" (he prefers a mid-grey neutral); lower saturation reads calmer. — [Feng Shui Modern summary](https://syedtalha.bearblog.dev/feng-shui-modern-by-cliff-tan-a-personalised-summary/)

### Inferences
- The house's own name printed on a mat sits squarely in the category the owner's consultant (Cliff Tan) advises against — not as a superstition about luck but as a matter of respect: a "regarded symbol" should not be trampled. The same logic is why LoveToKnow and Space Doula object to monograms. The counter-position (permanence, self-expression) is a retail voice, not a practitioner one.
- Practitioner-consistent alternatives if the name is to appear at all: put it on the signage plate (which the house already has), keep the mats plain, or use a quiet motif rather than the wordmark; if a mark is kept, a small, low-saturation one on a neutral ground is more defensible than a large name.
- The gold-dot/circle of the logo is, in shape terms, a Metal form (round, symmetrical) — harmonious at west doors and neutral elsewhere; it is only a problem under the "don't trample what you revere" rule if it counts as the brand's regarded symbol.

### Gaps
- No feng shui source discusses dark lettering on a light ground vs light on dark; any preference would rest on general yin/yang contrast reasoning, not on a citable rule.
- Chinese practice: e-commerce listings show red mats printed 出入平安 ("peace going out and coming in") are common, but I found **no** practitioner source discussing whether stepping on auspicious characters (福, 出入平安) is a concern — the search explicitly returned nothing on "福字不宜被踩."
- Cliff Tan's doormat advice is a single sentence in a corporate feature plus one line in a book summary; no video transcript on doormats specifically was found.

## 4. Who says what — classical sources vs the named modern authors (Lillian Too, Rodika Tchi, Anjie Cho, Marie Diamond, Cliff Tan, Chinese tradition)

### Takeaway
The named authors split cleanly: **Lillian Too / WOFS** (compass school) and **Master Sean Chan** (classical) discuss the *door* (Kua-keyed colour and shape, bright hall, poison arrows) and never the mat; **Anjie Cho** (BTB) gives the clearest practitioner mat advice (black or red, rectangular, door-width, clean, not thick); **Cliff Tan** adds the one principle that is distinctively his — nothing you admire goes underfoot — and frames entrances as first-impression design rather than luck; **Rodika Tchi's** and **Marie Diamond's** mat views could not be sourced.

### Cited Findings
- **Lillian Too / WOFS:** "The most important part of your home to attend to in terms of feng shui is your front door"; the door should be solid, not glass; "Main doors should face open space. This is known as the bright hall effect"; colour/shape by Kua table (West → round, white; East → rectangular, green/brown). No doormat guidance. — [WOFS](https://www.wofs.com/?p=5307)

## 5. West-facing sea-view houses and the bright hall (ming tang) at the entrance

### Takeaway
The bright hall is the open, uncluttered space in front of — and, secondarily, just inside — the main door where chi gathers before entering; it is about proportion and clarity, not lighting or size. A west-facing house is read as Metal with "excess yang" from the setting sun, to be balanced with earth tones, shading and soft light rather than amplified with fire colours. No source I reached treats a sea in front as a special case, and classical sources note the "facing" of a house may be its most active side (large glazing, view) rather than the street door — which matters for deciding which of this house's thresholds is the real mouth of chi.

### Cited Findings
- Definition: ming tang is "an open space in front of a property's facade or main door"; "The bigger the space, the 'brighter' it would be"; "The purpose of open spaces is for energy, or chi, to accumulate gently"; the area "directly inside the main door can often also be termed as a bright hall" (internal) — "desirable. But it's not essential"; the main door links the external and internal bright halls "in a smooth transition"; a 1:1 ratio of bright hall to house "would be more than sufficient"; light fixtures have "no bearing on bright hall no matter how many lumens"; a lamp post or pylon in it "can be very bad." — [fengshuied.com, bright hall](https://www.fengshuied.com/?p=2948)
- "Ming Tang is about proportion and clarity, not size"; it should be "open, bright, and not overloaded"; keep the entry clear, lit and deliberate. — [Fengshuiism wiki](https://fengshuiism.pages.dev/wiki/ming-tang)
- Paulynne Cheng (Bazi/Feng Shui practitioner, Malaysia): every home has two bright halls, "the space immediately outside and inside our front doors"; "Clutter in this area will inevitably result in stagnant Qi"; shoes belong in a cabinet; "In the practice of Feng Shui, the mirror has no impact." — [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/lifestyle/living/2023/05/07/what-you-need-to-know-about-bright-halls-in-feng-shui)
- WOFS: "Main doors should face open space. This is known as the bright hall effect and is most auspicious"; "A straight path that leads directly into your home sends slivers of killing energy" — prefer curved paths and lighting; outdoor furniture must never block the door. — [WOFS](https://www.wofs.com/?p=5307)
- West-facing house: "a west-facing house can have excessive yang energy because of the setting sun"; remedies — "shades or black-out curtains" for the brightest hours and landscaped tree borders; palette white/grey, metallics, and earth tones (beige, dusty yellow); avoid vibrant reds/oranges/purples. — [Homedit](https://www.homedit.com/feng-shui/west-facing-house)
- Cliff Tan: West is the Tiger, "where the Sun sets and Day turns to Night" — suits adult bedrooms, cooler colours and soft lighting; "Direction is determined not only by front door but by feel of the strongest feature like the largest windows"; "Water should always be moving. Stagnant water signifies decay"; prefer "Calming images of lakes or waterfalls, rather than stormy seas." — [Feng Shui Modern summary](https://syedtalha.bearblog.dev/feng-shui-modern-by-cliff-tan-a-personalised-summary/)
- Classical caveat reported in search results: the "facing" direction depends on the most active side of the home, not necessarily the front door (search summary; consistent with the Cliff Tan line above and with fengshuied's "assumes that the main door is aligned to be at the facing").

### Inferences
- For this house the external bright hall of the *street* door is the approach from the east; the terrace and garden to the west form a second, very large open space in front of the glazed wall. If the consultant reads the house as **west-facing** (sea, largest glazing, terrace), then the west threshold — not the street door — is the one the ming tang logic cares most about, and its mat colours should follow Metal/Earth (light stone, beige, grey), which the plan already does.
- The mat is the one object that sits exactly in the internal/external bright hall; everything the sources say about that zone (clear, flat, uncluttered, nothing to kick or trip on, no visual noise) reads directly onto "plain, flat, full-width, well kept" — and against a busy or wordy mat.

### Gaps
- No source discusses water/sea in the bright hall or a west sea view specifically; the classical "water in front of the facing" doctrine was not reached in these sources and is not cited here.
- Classical texts (Tang–Qing manuals) were not consulted directly; fengshuied and Sean Chan are practitioner summaries.

## 6. Applicability to the Paphos house — what the sources imply and what to ask the consultant

### Takeaway
Nothing in the planned scheme is "forbidden" by any school; the two points worth putting to Cliff Tan are (1) the house name printed on the mats — his own published rule is that nothing you admire belongs underfoot — and (2) whether he reads the house as east-facing (street door) or west-facing (sea/glazing), because that decides which threshold's colours matter and whether a light-grey base on the east door is a Metal-cuts-Wood mismatch or just stone.

### Inferences (door by door, as principle not superstition)
- **Front door, east, outside (travertine) and inside (marble):** Wood door. The dark-green mark is the on-element colour; a **khaki/beige base** is tolerated (Earth, listed "for support" by one source, controlled by Wood in the cycle — neutral at worst); a **light-grey base** is the one to query (Metal controls Wood), unless the consultant reads the stone grey as Earth. Rectangular, full door width, flat, two mats acceptable. The name on it is the real question — by Cliff Tan's rule, keep the wordmark for the carved entrance plate and let the mats be plain (or carry a quiet motif).
- **Kitchen side door:** secondary door; no school regulates its mat beyond clean/flat/unobstructed; match the house palette.
- **West terrace (sliding glass to garden/pool/sea):** Metal/Earth side. Light stone, beige and grey bases are exactly on-palette; green is the controlled element — acceptable as a small accent, not as a field; never red. If the consultant treats the west as the true facing, this is the threshold whose bright hall (terrace + garden) should stay clearest — the mat plain and generous.
- **Three bedroom balconies and the inside-balcony mat:** balconies sit on the "withdraw" floor Cliff Tan associates with the West/Tiger (adult bedrooms, cooler colours, soft light); sliding glazing is not a door in any school, so these mats are purely practical — keep them quiet, natural-fibre or flat-weave, no text needed.
- **Questions for Cliff Tan:** (1) Is the house east-facing (street door) or west-facing (sea/glazing) in his reading, and does that change the mat colours? (2) Does the house name on a mat fall under his "regarded symbol" rule, or is a small low-contrast mark acceptable? (3) Light grey on the east door — stone or Metal? (4) Any view on natural vs synthetic fibre at the sea-exposed west thresholds (salt air argues for synthetics; the yin/natural-fibre tradition argues the other way)?

### Gaps
- Actual compass bearings of the doors were not measured; "east" is the assignment's presumption. All directional readings above are conditional on it.
